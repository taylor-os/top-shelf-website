The panic call you miss is the customer who dials the next exterminator

A homeowner who just found a wasp nest by the front door, a mouse in the pantry, or bed bugs in the mattress is not going to leave a voicemail. They are anxious, they want it gone today, and they hang up and call the next exterminator on the list until someone picks up. Pest control runs on that panic more than almost any trade, because the customer is scared of what is in their home and about to let a stranger in to deal with it. But your techs are out on their routes, mid-treatment under a sink or up on a ladder, and the office line rings and rings, so the call rolls to voicemail and the job, along with the protection plan it could have become, goes to whoever answered.

An AI receptionist answers on the first ring, stays calm with a rattled caller, finds out whether it is stinging insects, rodents, bed bugs, or a routine quarterly visit, gets the address, and either books the stop or flags it to you as urgent on the spot. The job is captured and scheduled instead of lost to the company down the road.

Built around how a pest control company actually books work

Your calls do not keep office hours, and they do not all mean the same thing. A wasp nest by a swing set where the kids play needs someone today, bed bugs need an inspection and prep before treatment, and a quarterly service is just another stop on the route. A voicemail box cannot tell those apart, and a generic call center reading a script does not know a carpenter ant from a termite or which one can wait until next week.

  • Answers after hours and on weekends, when the wasp nest and the rat in the kitchen that turn into same-day jobs tend to show up.
  • Triages the way you would: stinging insects, rodents, roaches, bed bugs, or termites, one-time or recurring, and how fast someone needs to be there.
  • Gets the address, books routine and quarterly stops into the right area and day so a tech is not driving across town for one visit, and flags the true emergencies to you at once.
  • Knows a plan member from a first-time caller, so the customers already on a recurring agreement never hit a voicemail.

The math is one saved account

You do not need to catch many calls for this to pay for itself. A single panic call that turns into a recurring protection plan is worth far more than the one-time fee, because it bills every quarter for years, and one bed bug or termite job you would have lost on a weekend often covers the service for months. Everything it captures after that first save is on top. The point is to stop handing your best accounts to the exterminator who simply answered faster.

You own the number, the calls, and the customer list

This runs on your existing business number, or a new one registered in your name, not ours. Every caller, every address, and every job detail is yours and exportable any time, so the customer list, the thing that turns into recurring revenue, is an asset you keep instead of something you rent back month to month. There is no long contract holding your data hostage. The AI receptionist is one piece of the Top Shelf platform, and it hands every call it captures to the same CRM that follows up, so a quote or a plan renewal never slips.

How it plays out

A Saturday wasp nest, booked while you are on another route

It is Saturday afternoon and a homeowner finds a wasp nest right over the door their kids use. They search for an exterminator and call the first three they see. Two go to voicemail. Yours answers, stays calm, confirms nobody has been stung and where the nest is, gets the address, and books the stop for that afternoon while flagging it to your phone as urgent. Can it tell a real emergency from a routine service call?

Yes, that is the point. It asks the questions you would, stinging insects or rodents, bed bugs or termites, one-time or a scheduled quarterly visit, and treats a wasp nest by the door as urgent while booking a routine service into a normal slot. You set what counts as a drop-everything job.
