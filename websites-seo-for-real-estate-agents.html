Websites & SEO for Real Estate Agents

A real estate agent website built for SEO ranks for your name and your city, shows up before the portals in local search, and captures a buyer or seller's contact information the moment they land, so the traffic and the leads belong to you instead of Zillow or Realtor.com.

The portals are not competing with you, they are renting you back your own buyers

Zillow, Realtor.com, and Redfin outrank most individual agent sites because they publish thousands of pages a day and have years of authority behind them. A buyer searching for homes in your city, or even for a real estate agent in your city, usually lands on a portal first, and if they request information there, the portal decides which agent gets the lead, sometimes for a referral fee out of your own commission. Your site not ranking is not just a vanity problem. It is the reason a lead you should have gotten for free gets sold back to you, or handed to whichever agent is on rotation that week.

Rank for the searches only you can legitimately win

You will not outrank Zillow this year for a broad search like homes for sale in your metro, and you do not need to. You can rank for your own name, for the specific neighborhoods and subdivisions you work, and for searches like "[neighborhood] realtor" or "[city] listing agent," where a national portal has nothing local to say and your site can. Content built around the areas you actually sell in, past sales in those areas, and neighborhood detail a portal will never write is what search engines, and buyers, reward.

A visitor should not be able to leave your site anonymous

A property search or a home-value estimate tool that never asks for anything back is a nice feature and a wasted opportunity. The site should offer real value, listing search, a value estimate, a neighborhood guide, in exchange for a name and a way to reach them, so a visitor becomes a lead in your CRM instead of a number in your traffic report.

The traffic is yours, permanently

Every dollar and every write-up you put into a portal profile disappears the day you stop paying or the portal changes its rules. A website you own keeps ranking, keeps capturing leads, and keeps compounding in value for as long as it exists, and it is registered to you, not your brokerage or a portal account that vanishes the day you switch offices. The site plugs into the same CRM that follows up on every lead it captures, so nothing it earns you goes cold.

How it plays out

A buyer skips the portal and calls you instead

Someone searches for a real estate agent in a specific suburb and, instead of a Zillow ad, finds your site ranking with a guide to that exact suburb built from listings you have actually sold there. They try the home-value tool out of curiosity, leave their name and email, and your CRM follows up before they even finish browsing. You paid nothing per lead, and the portal never touched it.

Questions Real Estate Agents Ask

Common Questions

01

Do I still need Zillow and Realtor.com if I have my own site?

Probably yes, for now. Most buyers still check the portals. The point is not to replace them, it is to also own a channel where the leads are entirely yours instead of shared or sold.

02

Will my site actually outrank Zillow?

Not for broad searches like homes for sale in your city. It can realistically rank for your name, your specific neighborhoods, and long-tail local searches a national portal has no reason to target, which is exactly where an individual agent can win.

03

Does this work with my MLS or IDX listings?

Yes. Your active listings display on your own site with search built in, so a visitor can browse real inventory without leaving for a portal.

04

My brokerage already gave me a website. Why do I need another?

A brokerage template usually looks like every other agent at the firm and is not built or optimized for your specific market. This one is built to rank for the neighborhoods you work and to capture leads into your own CRM, not a shared brokerage system you do not control.

05

How long until it starts ranking?

Local, specific searches can start moving within weeks; broader terms take longer and compound over months. Setup is included with no separate fee, and a free audit will show you where your current site, or lack of one, stands today.
