The call you miss is the emergency that calls the next plumber

A homeowner with water spreading across the kitchen floor is not going to leave a voicemail. They hang up and call the next plumber on the list, and the one after that, until someone picks up. Plumbing runs on that kind of urgency more than almost any trade, because every minute the water runs is more damage to the floor, the ceiling below it, and the drywall. But you are under a sink with a wrench in your hand, in a crawlspace with no signal, or driving to the last job, so the call rolls to voicemail and the emergency, along with the premium after-hours ticket that comes with it, goes to whoever answered.

An AI receptionist answers on the first ring, stays calm with a panicked caller, finds out whether it is an active leak, a sewage backup, or no hot water, gets the address, and either books the visit or flags it to you as a true emergency on the spot. The job is captured and scheduled instead of lost to the shop down the road.

Built around how plumbers actually get calls

Your calls do not keep office hours. A water heater fails on a Sunday, a pipe bursts at 2am in a cold snap, a toilet overflows during dinner, and the calls that pay the most are the ones that come when every other shop is closed. A voicemail box cannot triage an emergency, and a generic call center reading a script does not know a slab leak from a running toilet, or which of the two can wait until morning.

  • Answers 24/7, including the 2am burst pipe and the holiday-weekend sewage backup that are your best-paying jobs.
  • Triages like you would: active leak or dry, water shut off or not, sewage or clean, no hot water or a slow drain, and how fast someone needs to be there.
  • Gets the address and the details, books routine work on your calendar, and texts you the true emergencies at once so you decide whether to roll a truck.
  • Handles a repeat customer or a maintenance-plan member differently from a first-time caller, so the people who already trust you never hit a voicemail.

The math is one emergency job

You do not need to catch many calls for this to pay for itself. One burst pipe or one water heater replacement you would have lost on a weekend is often worth more than the system costs for months, and after-hours emergency work is usually your highest-margin ticket of all. Everything it captures after that first save is on top. The point is to stop handing your best-paying calls to the plumber who simply answered faster.

You own the number, the calls, and the customer list

This runs on your existing business number, or a new one registered in your name, not ours. Every caller, every address, and every job detail is yours and exportable any time, so your customer list is an asset you keep instead of something you rent back month to month. There is no long contract holding your data hostage. The AI receptionist is one piece of the Top Shelf platform, and it hands every call it captures to the same CRM that follows up, so a quote or a maintenance reminder never slips.

How it plays out

A 2am burst pipe, booked while you sleep

It is 2am in the first hard freeze of the year and a homeowner wakes up to water coming through the ceiling. They search for a plumber and call the first three they see. Two go to voicemail. Yours answers, stays calm, confirms the water is now shut off at the main, gets the address, and books you for first thing in the morning while flagging it to your phone as urgent. You wake up to the job already on your calendar with the address, the problem, and the customer's number attached, instead of hearing about it after they hired someone else.

Questions Plumbers Ask

Common Questions

01

Does it work with my current phone number?

Yes. It can answer on your existing business number, or set up a new one registered in your name. Either way the number and every call that comes through it belong to you and go with you if you ever leave.

02

Can it tell a real emergency from a routine call?

Yes, that is the point. It asks the questions you would, is the water shut off, is it sewage or clean, is there hot water, and treats an active leak or a backed-up main as urgent while booking a dripping faucet or a quote as routine. You set what counts as a drop-everything emergency.

03

Will it actually book the job on my calendar?

Yes. Routine and scheduled work lands straight on your calendar based on your availability, and you get a text with the details right away. For a true emergency it can book the first open slot and alert you at once so you decide whether to roll a truck tonight.

04

Is it going to sound like a robot to a panicked customer?

It answers naturally and calmly, and it is upfront instead of pretending to be a person. Someone standing in a flooding kitchen mostly needs to know that a real plumber is handling it, and a steady voice that captures the details beats a voicemail box every time. You can hear it handle a live call before you decide.
