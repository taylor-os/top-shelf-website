The patients who dropped off their plan are the visits you are losing

Most chiropractic offices do not have a new-patient problem so much as a follow-up problem. A patient starts a care plan, feels better halfway through, and quietly stops booking. Life gets busy, the pain fades, and they never come back for the visits that would have kept them well, until it flares up again and they search for a chiropractor from scratch as if they never knew you. That patient was never really gone. They just needed a reason and a nudge to come back, and that is the thing no one at a busy front desk has time to do by hand.

A CRM keeps every one of those patients in front of you and reaches back out on a schedule you set, with texts and emails that go out on time whether or not anyone remembers. The patient who fell off at visit six hears from you before their back sends them somewhere else, and the table fills back up with someone who already trusts you.

Recall and reactivation is the goldmine in your files

The patients you have already treated are the cheapest visits a chiropractor can book. They know your table, they trust your hands, and their next flare-up, their spouse, and their kids are all yours if you stay in touch. But no one can personally remember to reach back out to a few thousand past patients, so most of that recurring work drifts to whoever they find online once they have forgotten your name.

  • Every patient, their visit history, and their care-plan status live in one place instead of a paper chart and memory.
  • Recall messages go out on schedule, a nudge when a patient is due and a check-in when one goes quiet, so people come back before their pain does.
  • You can see at a glance who has not been in for a while and reach the right patient with the right message at the right time.
  • A reactivation message to your inactive list can fill a slow week with patients who were always yours to bring back.

The lead that called but never booked is still yours to win

Not everyone books on the first call. Someone phones to ask whether you take their insurance, says they will check their schedule and call back, and then never does, not because they chose another office but because the day got away from them. A single follow-up a day or two later, a friendly note that you have a spot for them and can answer any question, is often all it takes to turn that maybe into a first visit. The CRM sends it for you, so the leads that used to evaporate turn into new patients on the schedule instead.

You own the list, and it works with the rest of the system

Every patient and every note is yours and exportable any time, not locked inside software you rent. The CRM is one piece of the Top Shelf platform and connects to the AI receptionist, so a new patient it books lands in your database and gets followed up on automatically, and to online booking, so a scheduled visit is logged against the right patient with their full history already attached. Nothing you have earned goes cold.

How it plays out

The patient who fell off their plan, back on the table

A patient comes in for lower-back pain, starts a care plan, and after a handful of visits feels good enough to stop booking. Normally that is the last you see of them until they hurt themselves again and start over with whoever comes up first on their phone. Instead, a few weeks after they go quiet, the CRM sends a warm check-in, and a gentle reminder a while later that you are here whenever they need a tune-up. When their back tightens up after a long weekend, yours is the name already in their phone, and they book with you without shopping around. No one at the front desk had to remember to chase them.

Common Questions

01

Does it import my existing patients and visit history?

Yes. Your current patients, leads, and visit history come in and live in one place, and everything stays yours and exportable. The point is to make the patient list you have already built actually work for you.

02

Can it bring back patients who dropped off their care plan?

Yes, that is where it earns its keep. It reaches patients who have gone quiet with recall and check-in messages on a schedule you set, so people come back for the visits that keep them well instead of waiting until the pain returns and searching again.

03

Will it follow up on new-patient leads that did not book?

Yes. When someone calls to ask about insurance or says they will call back, the CRM sends a friendly follow-up a day or two later for you, so the leads that used to slip away turn into first visits.

04

How is this different from the software I already chart in?

Charting software records the visit. It does not reach back out to a patient who stopped coming, remind you who is due, or tell you which lead is going cold. The CRM does all of that on a schedule, so the recurring visits actually show up instead of depending on anyone remembering.
