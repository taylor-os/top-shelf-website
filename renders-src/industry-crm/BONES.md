# Top Shelf CRM — shared bones for the industry screens

Owner direction (Taylor, 2026-11-26, verbatim): "I want all the different industries to look different, like they belong, for example the real estate one looks very different from the home service because they do different things, they still have customers and still need a CRM but there are different things that are more important to those business owners or employees that the other industry. ... I still want them using as much of the same "bones" or backend connectivity as possible to make each of these industries have a solid structure but the layout and what they see can be totally different based on the industry ... make the layouts and information they see on all the screens match what they are looking for, customers, owners, employees ect..."

So: **one CRM underneath, a different face per industry.** Every industry screen is built from the same records and the same visual kit, but each industry picks its own home screen, its own words, its own main workflow screen, and shows each role (owner, staff, customer) what that person checks first.

## The shared records (the bones). Every industry maps its own words onto these. Never invent a record type outside this list; rename and rearrange instead.

| Bone | What it holds | Example industry words |
|---|---|---|
| Customer | person/household/company, phones, emails, tags, notes, history, balance | guest, patient, client, member, buyer/seller, customer |
| Visit (calendar item) | a time-boxed thing on the schedule, with a staff member, a place (on site / in shop / chair / room / table) and a status | job, appointment, reservation, booking, showing, consult, class, repair order slot |
| Deal (pipeline card) | something moving through stages toward money | estimate, quote, listing, buyer search, matter, treatment plan, event/catering, car deal, membership sale |
| Work item | the thing being done, with line items, photos, notes, checklist | job ticket, repair order, chart-free visit note, matter task list, detail package, ticket/tab, order |
| Money | estimates, invoices, deposits, card payments, balances, tips, payroll/commission | check, retainer, copay balance, deposit, invoice |
| Conversation | one inbox of calls, texts and web chat per customer, with the AI receptionist and missed-call text-back writing into it | — |
| Automation | reminders, follow-ups, review requests, on-my-way / ready texts, rebook nudges | — |
| Team | users and roles, who sees what | owner, manager, front desk, tech, stylist, hygienist, paralegal, server, sales, coach |
| Customer view | what the customer sees from the business: booking page, customer hub (their estimates, invoices, pay link), text messages, the AI chatbot on the business's site | — |
| Website link | the business's website feeds leads/bookings into the CRM; some industries publish from the CRM to the site (listings, inventory, menu specials, class schedule) | — |

Signature plan features that must be visible somewhere across an industry's set where they make sense: AI receptionist that answers and books, missed-call text-back, online booking, schedule/dispatch board, customer hub, invoice by text, card payments, estimates, review requests and replies, on-my-way/ready texts, follow-up reminders, AI chatbot on their website. Don't show features that are not in that list (no POS registers, no payroll processing, no accounting ledgers, no marketing/ads/SEO tools, no EHR/charting, no e-signature, no MLS integration beyond "listing status").

## Visual bones (same for every industry)
The emerald Top Shelf CRM look from the homepage (`C:\Users\taylo\top-shelf-website-focus\assets\img\crm\hero-dash-d.jpg`, `hero-dash-m.jpg`): emerald sidebar/nav, emerald header band on desktop, light canvas, white cards, the same type, buttons, chips, icons and phone bottom-tab bar. Customer-facing screens (booking page, customer hub, pay page) are styled as the business's own page (its name and a modest accent), clearly not the staff app. What changes per industry: the nav items and their order, the home screen's layout and widgets, the main workflow screen (calendar vs floor plan vs board vs listings grid vs matter page...), the words, the data, and which role is shown.

## Screen set per industry (6 images)
1. Owner home, desktop: what the owner checks first in this industry.
2. The industry's signature workflow screen, desktop (the screen only this industry would have).
3. A staff member's screen for their real job, phone or desktop as fits that role (front desk, tech, stylist, hygienist, paralegal, server/host, salesperson, coach...).
4. A second staff/owner screen, phone: the thing they do on the go.
5. The customer's view, phone: booking page, customer hub, pay link or confirmation, styled as the business.
6. A text/call moment, phone: the AI receptionist or a text thread that fits the industry.
Each image carries a bottom strip: "Sample screens · sample data, not a real business".

## Sample data rules
Made-up DFW-plausible people and businesses only, 555 phone numbers, no real business or person names (never "Sarah Laos", "Ready Real Estate"). Medical-dental: appointment types only, no diagnoses. Legal: practice areas and matter names only, no sensitive case facts. Amounts plausible. Busy but believable; no lorem ipsum, no obvious repeats. Avoid alarming numbers (dozens of overdue items) that make the sample business look broken.
