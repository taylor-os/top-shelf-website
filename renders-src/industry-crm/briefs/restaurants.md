# Restaurants & Bars: screen brief

Industry slug: `restaurants`. Sells to: `industry-restaurants.html` (promises: AI receptionist answers mid-rush and books the table, missed-call text-back, online booking from the website straight into the CRM, host texts a confirmation before service, review request after the visit plus fast replies, a guest list the owner owns and can text).

What was wrong with the emerald draft and must not come back: "jobs"-style Today list of 8 reservations with a server name and a Done chip, dollar KPIs pulled from checks ("Booked today $2,514", "Collected", "tips", "Comped"), Payroll and Cash in the nav, Tasks and Documents tabs, Open / In progress / Completed statuses, prix fixe line items on a reservation, "#RES-1047" numbers. A real dinner book is 40 parties and 160 covers, not 8 rows, and the money this CRM touches is event deposits and event balances, not checks.

## 2. Roles and what they need first

**Owner / GM (Marisol), opens it at 3:30 PM before family meal, and again on her phone at 11 PM**
1. How many covers tonight and where the crunch is (covers per 15 minutes against the booking limit).
2. Who is coming that matters: regulars, VIPs, birthdays and anniversaries, allergies, big parties.
3. Private events: what's booked, what deposit is in, whose final headcount is due.
4. Reviews since yesterday, and any that need a reply.
5. What the phone did without her: calls the AI answered, tables it booked, missed calls texted back.
- Trust: the covers number matches what she knows the book looks like, and the 7:30 crunch is right there. Dismiss: anything that looks like a home-services app (jobs, techs, routes) or pretends to know her sales (checks, tips, comps). That's her POS's job and she knows it.

**Host (Talia), on a tablet at the stand, phone ringing, line at the door**
1. The floor: which tables are open, seated (and how long), reserved next, being cleared.
2. Who arrives in the next 30 minutes and whether they confirmed.
3. The waitlist: who's waiting, how long, what she quoted, and one tap to text "your table is ready."
4. Guest flags on the table and the reservation: allergy, occasion, regular, seating request.
5. Which open table fits which waiting party.
- Trust: seating a walk-in or texting the waitlist takes two taps. Dismiss: no floor plan, or a plain list where she has to do table math in her head.

**Events coordinator (Jules), desktop in the office, afternoons**
1. Every private event and large party by stage, inquiry to done.
2. Deposits: paid, not paid, how much.
3. Final headcounts due this week.
4. The event sheet: room, time, menu package, dietary notes, timeline.
5. The balance: send it by text after the event.
- Trust: one card holds the room, the count, the menu, the deposit and the texts with the host. Dismiss: it calls events "jobs" or "estimates" and has no room or headcount.

**Server (Jonah), phone in apron pocket, one hand, 10 seconds between tables**
1. My tables tonight and who's at each.
2. Allergies, impossible to miss.
3. The occasion (anniversary, birthday) and what the owner wants done about it.
4. Regulars and what they like.
5. Who sits at my open table next.
- Trust: the allergy is in red on the table card, not buried in a profile. Dismiss: it shows him money, phone numbers or anything he has to type into.

**Guest (Lauren, Nate), phone**
1. Is there a table for my party at the time I want.
2. If not, the closest times, or a waitlist.
3. A place to say "shellfish allergy" and "it's her birthday" once.
4. A text that confirms it and lets me reply instead of calling.
5. A real way to plan a bigger party.
- Trust: the booking page looks like the restaurant and the confirmation text arrives in seconds. Dismiss: a long form, an app download, or a card required for a table of 2.

## 6. Watch-outs

- **Stay out of the POS.** No checks, sales, average check, tips, comps, tabs, tickets, menu items or kitchen timing. The only dollars on staff screens are event proposals, deposits and balances (screens 1 and 3). The owner's tiles count covers, confirmations and reviews, not revenue.
- **Allergies are the guest's words, not a promise.** Show them loudly (red is reserved for allergies), but word them as notes: "Tree nut allergy (1)", never "nut-free meal guaranteed" or anything that sounds like a food-safety claim.
- **Server role sees no phone numbers, no money, no guest history beyond the note.** That's what makes an owner comfortable giving servers a login.
- **Event money must add up.** 30 x $80 = $2,400; +20% $480; 8.25% tax on $2,880 = $237.60; total $3,117.60; minus $750 = $2,367.60. Call it a "service charge", not a "tip" or "gratuity".
- **Keep the numbers consistent across screens:** 164 covers / 41 reservations / 22 in The Larder; Whitlock party of 6 at 7:30 on T16; Weller 7:00 on T12; Kim 7:15 on T5; Raman 7:30 on T6; waitlist of 4.
- **No alarming counts.** One review needs a reply, one final count is due, 8 unconfirmed out of 41. A Friday that looks busy, not broken.
- **Texts:** confirmation and reminder texts only go to guests who booked; the reserve page shows the reminder opt-in. No promo blast screens.
- **Photos on the reserve page:** food and room only, no visible signage, logos or text.
- **No real-kit conflict:** no restaurant kit exists yet, so these screens set the pattern. The industry page's example business is "The Copper Fox Kitchen & Bar" (Marco, 70 seats). Wrenfield is used here on purpose as a separate sample; if Taylor wants the screens to match the page story, swap the name and owner only, the rest holds.
