# Medical & Dental: screen brief (slug `medical-dental`)

Choice: **dental**, keeping **Alderbrook Family Dental**. Dental reads better than medical because the money levers the industry page sells (no-shows, recall, new-patient calls, reviews, texting) are sharpest in a hygiene-driven practice, and the page's own story is a dental office. The practice is shaped exactly like the page's example practice (3 chairs, 1 front-desk coordinator, 2 hygienists, owner dentist) so the screens prove that story, but it keeps its own name.

Sample day for every screen: **Tuesday, Dec 1, 2026** (avoid the draft's Fri Nov 27, the day after Thanksgiving).

## 2. Roles and what they need first

**Owner, Dr. Nadia Fairchild** (chairside all day; looks at 7 AM and again at lunch)
1. Are my three chairs full today, and where are the holes?
2. Is everyone confirmed, or am I about to eat a no-show?
3. New patients: how many this week, and did the lunch and after-hours calls get booked?
4. Recall: are this month's cleanings getting booked?
5. Money slipping away: patient balances, accepted estimates nobody booked, reviews waiting on a reply.
Trust: she sees her real day laid out chair by chair with the open hour and who can fill it. Dismiss: it looks like it wants to replace her practice software (charts, x-rays, claims), it puts health details in a text, or it talks about "jobs" and "techs".

**Front desk, Lupe Garza** (phone ringing, patient at the counter, someone checking out)
1. Who is calling right now: name, next visit, unconfirmed or not.
2. Who is here, who is checking out: collect a balance, is the next visit booked.
3. Tomorrow's unconfirmed patients, one tap to text again.
4. Open chair time and the ASAP list to fill it.
5. The recall list to work in quiet minutes; text replies waiting.
Trust: confirmations and replies all land in one inbox with one-tap texts. Dismiss: double entry, or anything that makes her re-key what's already in the practice software.

**Hygienist, Kendra Ellis** (between patients, gloves off for a minute)
1. My column today: who is next and are they here.
2. Book the next 6-month cleaning before the patient leaves the chair.
3. Family members who are due, so they come in together.
4. Am I running on time; is there a gap.
5. Hand the patient to the front desk ("ready for checkout").
Trust: two taps to book the 6-month visit. Dismiss: lots of typing, or money and billing clutter she doesn't handle.

**Patient, Megan Brennan** (from a text link on her phone)
1. When is my family's next visit, and is it confirmed?
2. Confirm or move it without calling.
3. What do I owe; pay it on my phone.
4. Book my son right after me.
5. Address, parking, hours.
Trust: the practice's own name on a secure page, and texts that never mention anything personal. Dismiss: download an app, create a password, or a text that reveals why she's coming in.

## 6. Watch-outs

- **HIPAA tone.** The page promises a HIPAA setup with a BAA and "no PHI in plain messages". So every text on every screen names the practice, a date and time, and a link or reply code, never an appointment type, procedure or reason. Recall texts say "your next checkup", not anything clinical. The "HIPAA setup · BAA on file" chip on screen 1 is the only compliance claim; don't add badges like "HIPAA certified".
- **Staff screens** may show appointment types, but only the seven listed. Balances show an amount and a visit date, never what the visit was for. No ages beyond a child's age next to the name; no birthdates.
- **Stay beside the practice software, not instead of it.** No charting, x-rays, tooth charts, perio, treatment notes, diagnoses, medical alerts, insurance claims or claim statuses. Insurance appears once, as "bring your insurance card".
- **Not home services.** No "jobs", no techs, no dispatch or on-my-way. The dental equivalent of "on my way" is "We're ready for you, come on in" for a patient waiting in the car. Remove Payroll, Cash and Mail from the draft nav.
- **Believable numbers.** 3 unconfirmed today, 5 for tomorrow, 2 open hours, 21 recall left to reach, $3,140 in balances across 14 patients. No red walls of overdue items, no no-shows on the sample day.
- **AI receptionist honesty.** Don't write patient lines that imply they think the AI is a person (no "the lady on the phone was so nice").
- **No real kit exists**, so these screens set the standard. The page's case-study practice is "Cedar Ridge Family Dental / Dr. Maria Alvarez"; the screens deliberately use Alderbrook with the same shape (3 chairs, 1 front desk, 2 hygienists). If Taylor wants the page and screens to match by name, rename one of them, don't mix.
- **Name check.** Alderbrook Family Dental and 4410 Juniper Bend Dr are made up; I couldn't confirm against a live directory, so search the name once before publishing. All phones are 555-01xx.
- **Dates.** Tue Dec 1, 2026 sample day; Jun 1, 2027 is also a Tuesday (the 6-month rebook).
