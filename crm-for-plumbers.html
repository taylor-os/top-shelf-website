The quotes you already sent are the jobs you are losing

Most plumbers do not have a lead problem. They have a follow-up problem. You quote a water heater replacement or a repipe, the homeowner says they need to think about it, and then you get busy and never circle back. They collect two other bids, and the job goes to whoever followed up, not necessarily the lowest price. The quote was never dead. It just needed one more call or text a few days later, and that is the thing there is never time for between service calls.

A CRM keeps every open quote in front of you and follows up on a schedule you set, with texts and emails that go out on time whether or not you remember. The homeowner deciding between three bids hears from you again while the other two go quiet, and the job comes back to you.

Every home you service is a customer for decades

Past customers are the cheapest work a plumber can get. You have already been in the house, they already trust you with a key and a crawlspace, and the next water heater, the next drain, the next remodel is yours if you stay in touch. But you cannot personally remember to reach back out to a few thousand past customers, so most of that repeat work goes to whoever they find on Google when they forget your name.

  • Every customer, address, job history, and note lives in one place instead of a truck full of paper invoices and your memory.
  • Maintenance reminders go out on schedule, a water heater flush, a drain cleaning, a sump pump check before storm season, so the work comes back around without you tracking dates.
  • You can see who has not called in a while and reach the right customer with the right reminder at the right time.

The customers who forgot your name are still yours to keep

A homeowner you helped two years ago is not disloyal when they call another plumber. They just lost your card and never saved your number. A steady, light touch, a seasonal tip, a maintenance reminder, a quick check that the last repair is still holding, keeps your name in their phone so the next emergency comes to you. It costs almost nothing, and it is the difference between a one-time job and a customer for the life of the house.

You own the list, and it works with the rest of the system

Every customer and every note is yours and exportable any time, not locked inside software you rent. The CRM is one piece of the Top Shelf platform and connects to the AI receptionist, so a call it answers lands in your database and gets followed up on automatically, and to online booking, so a scheduled visit is logged against the right customer with their full history already attached. Nothing you have earned goes cold.

How it plays out

The water heater quote that closes itself

You quote a homeowner on a new water heater on a Monday and they tell you they want to get another price. Normally that is the last you hear of it. Instead the CRM sends a friendly check-in two days later and a short note a few days after that, both written to sound like you. The other two plumbers never followed up, so when the homeowner is ready on Friday, yours is the only name still in front of them, and they book with you without shopping any further. You never sat down to chase it.

Illustrative example, not a client.

Questions Plumbers Ask

Common Questions

01

Does it import my existing customers and past jobs?

Yes. Your current customers, leads, and job history come in and live in one place, and everything stays yours and exportable. The point is to make the customer list you already have actually work for you.

02

Will it really follow up on quotes automatically?

Yes, on the schedule you approve. An open estimate gets a check-in a few days later and another after that, all sent for you, so a homeowner comparing bids keeps hearing from you while the other plumbers go quiet. You can jump in and message anyone directly any time.

03

Can it send maintenance reminders?

Yes. You set the cadence, a water heater flush each year, a drain cleaning, a sump pump check before the rainy season, and the reminders go out automatically so recurring work comes back around without you tracking every date.

04

How is this different from just having their number in my phone?

A phone full of contacts does not follow up, does not remember who is due for maintenance, and does not tell you which quote is going cold. The CRM does all of that on a schedule, so the repeat work actually shows up instead of depending on you to remember.
