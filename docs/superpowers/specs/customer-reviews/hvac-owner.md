# Customer review: Dwayne, HVAC owner, Plano (`hvac-owner`)

**Who I am:** 46, I own a heating and air company in Plano. 6 techs, 2 office staff, me. About 1,400 past customers split between a spreadsheet and an old field-service app. I ran Housecall Pro until the price went up and I walked. I look at this stuff on my phone in the truck between calls, so if it doesn't work on a phone it doesn't work.

**What I looked at:** the current site (home, CRM, websites, CRM by industry, pricing, the home services page, and the "See your business" pop-up), then Concepts A, B and C (home, CRM, websites, industries, and the home-services CRM and website pages). Phone at 390x844 first, then desktop at 1440x900. Screenshots are in the scratchpad under `customer-reviews\hvac-owner\`.

**My crew is 9 people, so I'm in the $99 tier. With one phone line I'd pay $149 a month.** Every version got that across. The trouble is what comes after the price.

---

## Current site (updated)

**1. First 10 seconds on the phone.** I get it. "Run the whole business from one app," and right next to the button: "From $99 a month. No setup fee. No contract." That's the best price placement of the four because it sits beside the button, not under it. The dashboard below says "Good morning, Travis" with real-looking money numbers, and the line "The screens are from the real app, shown with a sample HVAC company" made me look twice, in a good way. I'd keep scrolling.

**2. Built for me?** The screens are HVAC all the way: a run capacitor 45/5 MFD on the estimate, R-410A recharge, "AC not cooling, upstairs unit" on a Plano address. Whoever built that knows the trade. The home-services page is another story. The top is good ("You Didn't Start a Business to Be the Receptionist" is the truth), but then I get three different made-up companies on one site: Travis on the homepage, Marcus at "Ridgeline Comfort & Repair" in the story, and Ray at "Everline Home Services" in the screens. I'm trying to picture my company and they keep changing the company on me. Then the page runs forever. On the phone it's about 37,000 pixels long, and the back half is a wall of links: fence companies, pool service, window cleaning, 20-some trades, each with "AI receptionist / CRM & follow-up / Websites." That's for Google, not for me. By the time I hit "Junk removal companies" I've forgotten what I came for.

**3. Trust.** What helps: the price is clear, it says month to month and no setup fee more than once, and "Your customer data is yours... you can take it with you" on pricing. The owner quotes are the only proof on any of the four versions. One is a garage door guy, a trade like mine. The "hire it yourself" table ($4,520 a month against $99) is the kind of math I'd do anyway. What hurts: the CRM page answers six real questions with "We go over that on your demo call." Bring my customer list over? Demo call. QuickBooks? Demo call. Do techs only see their own jobs? Demo call. Who sets it up? Demo call. Support hours? Demo call. Housecall Pro's salespeople also told me everything on the call, and the bill still went up. On the phone, the "See your business" tab sits right on top of the "Call / Always answered" button in the bottom bar, so the one button I'd actually press gets covered.

**4. Unanswered.** Who moves my 1,400 customers and what does that cost? What's the card processing fee? What counts as "normal use" on the $50 line? Is the $99 locked, or can it go up next year? Does it handle maintenance agreements? Does the tech app work in an attic with no signal?

**5. Try the CRM?** Maybe. The pop-up, the stats and the quotes made me want to see it. The pile of "ask us on the call" answers made me want to wait.

**6. Favorite / least favorite.** Favorite: real owner quotes, and the sticky Call / Text bar at the bottom of the phone screen. Least favorite: the industry page's link wall, plus three fake HVAC companies on one site.

**The pop-up:** It opened after about half the page, which was right after I'd scrolled past the same industry grid. On the phone it takes the whole screen. Home Services was already picked, which was nice. Mostly it felt like an interruption that showed me what I'd just seen.

## Concept A (light, blue)

**1. First 10 seconds on the phone.** Clean and easy to read in daylight, which matters in a truck. Headline, two buttons, then four check marks: $99 for up to 10 users, no setup fee, month to month, websites $499. I knew what it was and what it cost before my thumb moved. Kept scrolling.

**2. Built for me?** Halfway. The home-services page is tight and short: one company (Everline), "Home services: from the 2 AM call to the paid invoice," a "What owners get" box with four lines, then the screens. Good/better/best estimates, a tip that goes to the tech, tax on materials only. Those are details from my world. On the homepage, though, "Get paid from their phone" shows a car lot payment screen and "Estimates by text" shows BMW brake pads from an auto shop. If I'm reading about estimates, show me a heat pump estimate.

**3. Trust.** The "How it works" block (1. Tap Try the CRM, 2. We call you, 3. Send your first invoice) is the only place on any version that tells me what happens after I hand over my number, and it ends on me sending an invoice, not on a sales pitch. "What you won't run into" with check marks reads well. "The same price every month" is the line I was looking for after Housecall Pro, but it's one bullet and it doesn't say for how long. The "Built so Google can read it... It isn't an ongoing SEO service" line is honest, and I respect that. No owner quotes anywhere. That's a big hole.

**4. Unanswered.** Same list: import, QuickBooks, card fees, price lock, maintenance agreements, who I talk to when something breaks.

**5. Try the CRM?** Yes, likely. It's the one that felt the least like a pitch and told me the steps.

**6. Favorite / least favorite.** Favorite: the 3-step "How it works." Least favorite: no proof from a single real customer, and car lot screens selling me estimates.

---

## Concept B (dark, purple)

**1. First 10 seconds on the phone.** Big bold headline, purple Try the CRM button, price bullets under the buttons. Clear enough. The dark purple reads more like an app startup than somebody who wants to run my office, but it's sharp.

**2. Built for me?** This one tells my day best. The homepage CRM section follows one Everline call through all five steps: "A call comes in" (the 2 AM no-heat call with the AI summary: address, "furnace stopped overnight, no heat upstairs"), "It's booked" (dispatch board), "The estimate goes out" (repair the capacitor for $289 or a new heat pump for $10,405), "They pay from their phone" (cash, check, Zelle, Venmo, card, tip 100% to the tech), "You ask for the review." On a phone those screens are big enough to actually read. That's the first time I thought "that's my Tuesday." The home-services page shows the same screens large, then a Signature plan box.

**3. Trust.** Price is clear and repeated. The industry list on the phone is 12 big cards stacked, so it's a long scroll to get past the car lots and law firms. There are no owner quotes and no "how it works" steps. It looks good, but nothing on it proves anyone uses it.

**4. Unanswered.** Same list as above.

**5. Try the CRM?** Maybe leaning yes. The screens sold me on the software. Nothing sold me on the company.

**6. Favorite / least favorite.** Favorite: the one-call story, 01 through 05, with readable screens. Least favorite: no proof and no steps, plus the startup look.

---

## Concept C (cream, red, newspaper style)

**1. First 10 seconds on the phone.** The top bar says "Based in DFW, Texas. (469) 833-3033" before anything else, and I like that. But the logo, menu and a Try the CRM button take up the first third of the screen, and the price doesn't show until I scroll. Lots of body text. It reads like a magazine, and I don't read magazines in the truck.

**2. Built for me?** The industry page is called "A day at Everline Home Services," which is a nice touch, and it has the same screens as A and B. On the homepage, the CRM section is a list of words with no pictures, so I had to take their word for it until I hit the industry grid.

**3. Trust.** The FAQ answers are open on the page. I don't have to tap five plus signs, and that's how answers should be. But "What you won't run into" uses red X marks, so at a glance it reads "X No contract, X No setup fee, X The same price every month." The first time through I read that as "not the same price every month." On a pricing section, that's a mistake that costs the sale.

**4. Unanswered.** Same list.

**5. Try the CRM?** Maybe. It feels local and honest, but it made me work too hard.

**6. Favorite / least favorite.** Favorite: open FAQ answers and the DFW phone number up top. Least favorite: red X marks on the good news, and a text-only CRM section.

---

## My ranking

1. **Concept A.** Easiest to read on a phone, price up front, the only one that tells me the steps, short industry page with one company.
2. **Concept B.** Best story of an HVAC day and the most readable screens, but it proves nothing about the company.
3. **Current site.** Has the only real proof and the best phone bottom bar, but the industry page is buried under SEO links, three fake companies and a pop-up that covers the Call button.
4. **Concept C.** Honest and local, but slow on the phone, and the red X marks confused me on price.

## 8 changes I'd make to Concept A

1. **Answer the six "demo call" questions on the page, in plain numbers.** CRM page FAQ and home "Straight answers." Bring my customer list over: say who does the import, what format (spreadsheet, export from Housecall Pro or Jobber), whether it's free, how many days. QuickBooks: yes, no or "export only." Techs see only their jobs: yes or no. Setup: who and how long. Support: hours and whether a person answers. Every "we'll tell you on the call" reads to me like "the answer is bad." *(steal C's open FAQ answers, no tapping)*

2. **Put a real price-lock promise next to the price.** Pricing card, under $99. "The same price every month" isn't enough for someone who left Housecall Pro over a hike. Say how long the $99 is locked, or say "we'll give you 60 days' notice before any change and you can leave free." This is the line that would make me tap.

3. **Show card processing fees and what "normal use" means on the phone line.** Pricing section, phone line card and the "Get paid" card. Card fees are where field-service software quietly makes its money. Give the rate. For the $50 line, give the minutes and texts included and the overage price. Right now "usage above that is billed" is a blank check.

4. **Bring back owner quotes, and put the home-services one on the home-services page.** Between pricing and the final Try the CRM. A has no proof at all. The current site's garage door owner quote ("every call gets answered and the job's booked before I'm back in the truck") is the best sentence on any version for a guy like me. Add a name, a city and how long they've been on it. *(steal from current site)*

5. **Use one HVAC call to walk the homepage CRM section, with phone-size screens.** "What the CRM handles for you." Swap the car lot payment and BMW estimate cards for B's 01 to 05 Everline story: 2 AM call, booked, estimate with repair vs. heat pump, paid at the door, review. Let the screens fill the phone width so I can read them. *(steal from B)* If you want other industries, let the industry picker swap the story.

6. **Keep one sample company per industry, everywhere.** Home hero, industry page, pop-up. The current site has Travis, Marcus at Ridgeline and Ray at Everline. Pick Everline and use it in the hero, the story and the screens, so I can follow one company the whole way and picture mine in its place.

7. **Add a "Switching from Housecall Pro / Jobber / ServiceTitan" block.** Home, under How it works, and on the home-services page. The current site has a "Switching from Hibu/Thryv" page, but nobody in HVAC uses Hibu. Tell me what moves over (customers, job history, price book, open estimates), what doesn't, and that I can run both side by side for a month. That's the real fear: switching mid-season and losing jobs.

8. **Add a sticky Call / Text bar on the phone, and never cover it.** Bottom of every page on mobile. A doesn't have one, and the current site has one but lets the "See your business" tab sit on the Call button. I check this stuff between jobs. One thumb, one tap to call a person. *(steal from current site, fix the overlap)*

**Also worth stealing:**
- From C: the "Based in DFW, Texas" phone number in the top bar. Local matters to me.
- From current: "The screens are from the real app" under the hero, the "hire it yourself" cost table, and the "your customer data is yours, take it with you" line on pricing.
- On the home-services page, mention maintenance agreements and memberships. That's half my revenue in the shoulder months, and the only place it shows up today is as a blog title in the link wall.
- Don't copy the current industry page's 20-trade link wall onto A. If Google needs it, put it on its own page.

---

## Would I tap Try the CRM?

On Concept A, **yes**, because it told me the three steps and the price without games. I'd tap it hoping the call answers the import, card fee and price-lock questions. If those three were answered on the page, I wouldn't need the call to decide. I'd just want to see it.
