# Customer review: Monique, salon and spa owner, Frisco (`salon-owner`)

**Who I am:** 38, I own a salon and spa in Frisco. 7 stylists, 1 booth renter, an esthetician and a front desk girl. We run on Vagaro. My clients find me on Instagram and book through the link in my bio or by texting me. What keeps me up: no-shows on a 3-hour color, deposits nobody collects, and first-timers who never rebook. I have been sold "all-in-one" software twice. One locked me into a year, the other never finished setting me up.

**How I looked:** every page on a phone (390x844) first, then on my laptop (1440x900). I scrolled, waited for the "See your business" pop-up, tapped Salon & Spa, opened the Lumen Wellness sample site and the salon CRM pages, and zoomed into the sample screens. Screenshots are in `C:\Users\taylo\AppData\Local\Temp\claude\e8a30302c63b0c61\503746ae-8215-4629-8d24-dc2bfa57ce43\scratchpad\customer-reviews\salon-owner\` (`cur_*`, `a_*`, `b_*`, `c_*`, plus `x_*` for the pop-up and Lumen site, `zz_*` for zoomed salon screens).

**The thing I need to say first:** the salon sample screens are the best sales tool on this whole site, and every version hides them. When I zoomed in on the Lumen Wellness "Owner, Today" screen, it was basically my salon: a row per stylist, a **booth renter** with her own book, a nail tech, an esthetician, "Due back: not booked and past their usual time", birthdays, a drawer count, and a **Booth rent: $325 weekly, paid** card. The booking screen says "A $25 deposit holds your time... cancel up to 48 hours before... if you miss it, the fee is 50%." Checkout has card on file, tip split by stylist, and "Book next visit: usually every 10 weeks." The stylist screen has the color formula and before/after photos. That is exactly what I'd buy. But in words, every version only promises "a calendar by chair", "online booking", "client texts in one thread" and "follow-up reminders". The words undersell the screens by a mile, and on a phone the screens are too small to read and don't open bigger when I tap them.

## Current site (updated)

**1. First 10 seconds on the phone.** Clear. "Run the whole business from one app", Try the CRM, and "From $99 a month, No setup fee. No contract." right next to the button. I know what it costs before I scroll. Then the first screen I see is "Good morning, Travis" and the note says it's a sample HVAC company. So in second 11 I'm thinking "this is for air conditioner guys." I kept scrolling only because the price was good.

**2. Built for a business like mine?** The homepage, CRM page and pricing page are written for trades: "jobs", "dispatch", "before you leave the driveway", "your tech sends an on-my-way text". Nobody in my salon drives to a driveway. The salon industry page is the most "me" thing on the whole site: "The Saturday no-show you could've filled five times", "the 2pm ghost", "the first-timer who loved it and never came back". Those made me nod. But then it is a **salon, spa and fitness** page, so half of it is about gym members who quit by month three, and the gaps list says "the quotes that go cold". I don't give quotes. The day-in-the-life story is about "Willow & Sage Studio", but the sample site and CRM are "Lumen Wellness", so I wasn't sure which one was the example. The Lumen sample website itself is lovely: calm, cream and plum, "Book a visit" up top, 4.9 stars. I'd be happy if mine looked like that. Deposits are never mentioned in the words on this page at all.

**3. Trust builders and killers.**
- Builds: "$99 for 1 to 10 users, $199 for 11 to 25", "Month to month", "No setup fee", "Your customer data is yours... you can take it with you." The pricing page FAQ is honest. The "Every piece is someone's full-time job" table is fine, but I already pay a front desk person and I'm not firing her for a robot.
- Kills: the three testimonials are a garage door company, a contractor and a car lot. No salon, no spa. The stat call-outs with little website names under them ("noshowcost.com", "regulr.ai") read like a blog post, not like someone who has stood behind a chair. The pages are very long (the salon page was about 30 phone screens, the CRM page even longer). The "See your business" pop-up takes over my whole phone screen halfway down, and on the salon page it opens on **Home Services** instead of Salon & Spa, even though I'm clearly on the salon page.

**4. Unanswered questions.** Can you move my clients, future appointments, cards on file, gift cards and packages out of Vagaro? What do you charge per card swipe? Does my booth renter count as one of my 10 users, and can she keep her clients private? Does the front desk count? (Me + 7 + 1 + esthetician + front desk is 11. Am I $99 or $199?) Does it work with the "Book" button on Instagram? Who sets it up and how long does it take?

**5. Would I tap Try the CRM?** Maybe. The price and no-contract terms got me close, but "Try" really means "fill out a form and we call you". I'm behind the chair all day. I'd rather text.

**6. Favorite / least favorite.** Favorite: the salon page's pain stories, and the Lumen sample site. Least favorite: everything on the homepage and CRM page assumes I'm an HVAC company.

## Concept A (clean white, blue buttons)

**1. First 10 seconds on the phone.** The clearest of the four. Headline, Try the CRM, Call or text, then four check marks all on the first screen: "From $99 a month for up to 10 users", "No setup fee", "Month to month, no contract", "Websites from $499, one time." I'd keep scrolling. The word "from" on the website price makes me wonder what makes it cost more than $499. The other pages say "$499 one time" flat.

**2. Built for a business like mine?** The homepage examples are still home services, a car lot and an auto shop. The industry picker on the homepage is nice: tap Salon & Spa and it says "Salon and spa: the whole book at a glance" with a calendar by chair and follow-ups for clients who are due back. The salon CRM page is short and to the point, and the desktop version shows the screens big enough to read. On the phone the stylist, booking and texts screens are side by side and too small. The website and CRM look like the same business (same plum color, same Lumen name), and that pairing makes sense to me.

**3. Trust builders and killers.**
- Builds: "How it works" in three steps (tap, we call you, send your first invoice). That told me what happens next, which is the thing I always worry about. Pricing is in the top menu. The salon CRM page has the $99 price right there at the bottom. "What you won't run into" list in the price card.
- Kills: it looks like every software company I've ever been pitched. Blue and white, very "app". No faces, no names, no salon owner saying it works. Nothing tells me there's a real person in DFW I'd be talking to except the footer.

**4. Unanswered questions.** Same as above: Vagaro move, card fees, booth renter seat, Instagram booking, who sets it up, how long. Also, step 3 says "send your first invoice." I don't send invoices. I check people out.

**5. Would I tap Try the CRM?** Maybe, leaning yes after the salon page. The "How it works" steps lower the fear.

**6. Favorite / least favorite.** Favorite: "How it works" and the price check marks above the fold. Least favorite: it feels generic and cold. Nothing about it says "beauty".

## Concept B (dark, purple, techy)

**1. First 10 seconds on the phone.** Price and terms are on the first screen, same as A. But it looks like a crypto app or a developer tool: black, purple glow, grid lines, typewriter labels. My first feeling was "this isn't for me."

**2. Built for a business like mine?** The salon CRM page is actually the best one on the phone, because the stylist, booking and texts screens are stacked one per row and big enough that I could read the deposit rule and the "C to confirm" text. The rest of the site is trades-first like the others. The dark background fights with the soft plum salon screens. There's also a big empty gap on the salon page between the buttons and the first screen.

**3. Trust builders and killers.**
- Builds: the numbered story on the homepage ("A call comes in, It's booked, The estimate goes out, They pay from their phone, You ask for the review") is easy to follow. The Signature plan card on the salon page with $99 and "See its website".
- Kills: no Pricing in the menu. When I scrolled to the price section the big "$99" was still blurred out (it seems to fade in late). A price I can't read for a second feels like a trick, even if it isn't. The preview badge sat on top of the salon page text. It feels like a tech startup that might not be around next year.

**4. Unanswered questions.** All the same ones. Plus: if I call that number, is it a real person in Texas?

**5. Would I tap Try the CRM?** No, not from this design. The salon screens almost won me back, but the look told me it was built for someone else.

**6. Favorite / least favorite.** Favorite: readable phone screens on the salon page. Least favorite: the overall dark tech look.

## Concept C (warm cream paper, red buttons, serif)

**1. First 10 seconds on the phone.** I get what they sell. The very top line says "Based in DFW, Texas. (469) 833-3033" and that made me relax a little. But the header is tall (logo, menu, then a Try the CRM button stacked), so the price ("From $99 a month for up to 10 users") is right at the bottom edge of my first screen and the rest of the check marks are below. I had to scroll to see "no contract". I kept scrolling because it felt like a local shop, not a software company.

**2. Built for a business like mine?** This one looks the most like my world. Cream, serif headings, a little hand-drawn underline. It looks like a boutique's website, and the Lumen sample site fits right in next to it. The homepage shows each industry as website and CRM side by side with "Website" and "CRM" links, which is the clearest version of "these go together". The salon page is called "A day at Lumen Wellness", and that framing is right for a salon. But on the phone the screens are tiny again (two to a row), and there's no price on the salon CRM page at all.

**3. Trust builders and killers.**
- Builds: "Based in DFW" at the top of every page. "What you won't run into: No contract. No setup fee. No per-user fee for up to 10 users. The same price every month. A website that's $499 one time, not another monthly bill." That list is written for someone who got burned, which is me. The Q and A format is easy to scan. The $99 is circled like someone marked it with a pen.
- Kills: no Pricing in the menu. No people, no faces, no salon owner quote. The salon page ends with "Next: A day at Ember & Oak", a restaurant, when I wanted "here's what it costs and how to get mine."

**4. Unanswered questions.** Same list. This version made me want answers more, because it was the first one where I pictured my salon in it.

**5. Would I tap Try the CRM?** Yes, probably. The warmth and the "won't run into" list got me there. I'd still rather text than get called.

**6. Favorite / least favorite.** Favorite: the look, and the "What you won't run into" list. Least favorite: tiny screens on the salon page and no price on it.

## My ranking

1. **Concept C.** Feels local and boutique, looks like it belongs next to a salon, and talks to people who've been burned.
2. **Concept A.** Clearest and fastest. Best "how it works". Just generic and cold.
3. **Current site.** Has the best salon-specific writing (the no-show and rebook stories) and the best price line on the first phone screen, but it is long, HVAC-first, and the salon page is mixed up with gyms.
4. **Concept B.** Best readable salon screens on a phone, wrong look for my industry, and the price fades in late.

## 8 changes I'd make to Concept C

1. **Salon CRM page, "What owners get" list: say what the screens already show.** Replace the four generic bullets with the real ones: "Deposits at booking, with your own cancel window and no-show fee", "Card on file at checkout", "Book the next visit before she leaves, based on her usual timing", "Color formulas and before/after photos on the client", "Tip split by stylist", "Booth renters on the book, with booth rent tracked". Why: deposits, no-shows and rebooking are the three reasons I'd switch. Right now I only found them by zooming into a screenshot.

2. **Salon CRM page, sample screens: make them readable on a phone.** Stack them one per row like Concept B does, and let me tap any screen to open it full size and pinch to zoom. *(Steal from B: the stacked phone screens. Steal from the current homepage: "Tap any card to see the screen up close.")* Why: the "Owner, Today" screen with the booth renter and "Due back" list is what sold me, and on a phone it's a blur.

3. **Add a "Switching from Vagaro, Square, Booksy or GlossGenius" block to the salon pages and the FAQ.** Say plainly what comes over (clients, history, future appointments, cards on file, gift cards, packages), who does it, and how long it takes. Why: I can't stop booking for a week. If moving my book sounds hard, I won't even take the call. The current site has "Switching from Hibu/Thryv" in the footer, so this kind of page already exists for other businesses.

4. **Pricing, everywhere: answer "who counts as a user" and "what do you charge per card".** One line: whether booth renters and front desk count toward the 10, and the card processing rate (or "the same rate you pay now" if that's true). Put Pricing back in the top menu. *(Steal from A: Pricing in the nav and the $99 band at the bottom of the industry page.)* Why: my team is 11 with me. The difference between $99 and $199 matters, and card fees matter more than either.

5. **First phone screen: get the price and "no contract" above the fold.** Shrink the stacked header (logo, menu and button on one line) so all four check marks show on the first screen. *(Steal from A: the four check marks right under the buttons. Steal from current: "From $99 a month, No setup fee. No contract." next to the button.)* Also change "Websites from $499" to "Websites $499, one time" so I don't wonder what makes it cost more.

6. **Give the salon its own page, not "salon, spa and fitness".** Keep the current site's salon stories ("the 2pm ghost", "the first-timer who never came back", "the bookings you sleep through") but drop the gym section and the "quotes that go cold" line, and use Lumen Wellness in the story so it matches the sample site and CRM. *(Steal from current: the pain stories, rewritten to match C's "A day at" framing.)* Why: the moment I read about gym memberships or quotes, I'm reminded it's a template.

7. **Add a "How it works" strip and a "text us instead" option.** Three steps like Concept A, but written for a salon: "Text or fill out the form", "We walk you through it on your schedule, after close is fine", "We set up your book and you take your first deposit." Next to Try the CRM, offer "Text us" as an equal choice. *(Steal from A: How it works.)* Why: "We call you" scares me. I'm with a client 9 to 7. And I'd honestly rename the button. "Try" makes me expect to play with it right now, and I can't.

8. **Put real people on it, and a beauty owner if you have one.** A face and first name for who I'd talk to, "Based in DFW" kept up top, and at least one quote from a salon, spa, barber or nail owner. If you don't have one yet, show the owners you do have, but say what kind of business they are. *(Steal from current: the owner testimonials, but add a beauty one.)* Why: three trades quotes tell me salons aren't your customers yet. A face tells me who to call when something breaks on a Saturday.

**Also worth fixing:** on the current site, the "See your business" pop-up on the salon page should open on Salon & Spa, not Home Services. And the salon CRM page in C should end with the price and Try the CRM, not "Next: A day at Ember & Oak."

---

## Would I tap "Try the CRM"?

On Concept C, yes, probably, after I saw the salon screens up close. On the current site and A, maybe. On B, no. On all four, I'd tap faster if I could text instead of waiting for a call, and if I knew my Vagaro book could come with me.
