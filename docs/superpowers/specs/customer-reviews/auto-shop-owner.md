# Customer review: Luis, auto repair shop owner (auto-shop-owner)

**Who I am:** Luis, 52. I own a 5-bay independent repair shop in Garland. Two service advisors, four techs. General repair, brakes, A/C, diagnostics, mostly Toyotas, Hondas, F-150s and Silverados, some fleet vans. I run Shopmonkey today and pay more than I want to for it. The thing I care about most is customers approving the estimate by text, line by line, so my advisors aren't stuck on the phone. I don't trust anything that sounds like a brochure.

**How I looked:** Phone first (390x844), then desktop (1440x900). Every home page, the CRM, websites and industries pages, and my two pages (current `industry-auto.html`, concepts `industries/auto-repair-crm.html` and `-website.html`). On the current site I waited for the "See your business" pop-up and picked Auto Repair. On Concept A I tapped Auto Repair in the home page picker. Screenshots are in the scratchpad folder `customer-reviews\auto-shop-owner\`.

---

## Current site (updated)

**1. First 10 seconds on the phone.** I get it. "Run the whole business from one app", Try the CRM, "From $99 a month, No setup fee. No contract." all on the first screen. Then it says the screens are from "a sample HVAC company." I'm not HVAC. I'd keep scrolling, but only because the price is low enough to be curious. The gold and black looks like a cigar lounge, not a shop.

**2. Built for a business like mine?** The auto page (`industry-auto.html`) is the most auto-specific of everything I saw. "The call you miss wrist-deep in an engine" is true. The declined-work follow-up section is the best thing on the whole site: struts and rear brakes he wasn't ready for, nobody called him back. That is real money in my shop. But then it introduces "Redline Auto & Tire" and "Mike", and further down the sample shop is "Apex Auto Werks", a BMW performance shop. Two made-up shops on one page and neither looks like mine. And the thing I care about most, the customer approving each job from a text, is never shown. It just says "send the estimate by text."

**3. Trust.** Builds it: the pricing page is clear ($99 for 1 to 10, $199 for 11 to 25, $50 a line, $499 site), and it says in plain words that my customer list, number and domain are mine and go with me if I leave. There are owner quotes with business names. Kills it: the "$4,520+/mo vs $99" table. Nobody in my shop costs $3,390 a month to answer phones and I've never paid a "reputation service." That's a sales trick. The stat boxes (21%, 38%, 97%, 30%+ with website names under them) read like an ad agency wrote them. The only auto quote is from a car lot (Reids), not a repair shop.

**4. Unanswered.** Does it replace Shopmonkey or sit next to it? Can I move my customers, vehicles and repair history over? Parts ordering, labor times, VIN lookup, inspections with photos? What's the card rate? What does "normal use" on the phone line mean in minutes and texts?

**5. Try the CRM?** Maybe. Price and no contract get me close. The auto page talks more than it shows.

**6. Favorite / least favorite.** Favorite: the declined-work follow-up story. Least favorite: the fake-math cost table and the wall of stats.

## Concept A (clean white and blue)

**1. First 10 seconds on the phone.** Best of the four. Headline, Try the CRM, the phone number as a real button, then four check marks: $99 a month for up to 10 users, no setup fee, month to month, websites from $499. I know what it is and what it costs before my thumb moves. I'd keep scrolling.

**2. Built for a business like mine?** Yes, once I find it. On the home page I tapped "Auto Repair" in the picker and it swapped to Apex Auto Werks: "every car on one board, every estimate by text," shop board, line-by-line estimates, card at pickup, calls answered while everyone's under a hood. That's my day. The auto CRM page shows the screens I care about: shop board with cars by stage and "waiting on parts," the repair order with labor and parts and approved / not answered / declined, a tech screen with no prices or customer names (good, my techs don't need to see that), and the car owner's estimate with Approve and Decline on each job and photos of their own worn pads and rotors. That last screen is exactly what I want. The problem: on my phone the screens sit two across and are too small to read. I had to squint to see the Approve buttons. Apex is still a BMW shop ("Dealer-level care for your BMW"). I don't work on BMWs much.

**3. Trust.** Builds it: price everywhere, including the auto page ($99 a month, 1 to 10 users). "What you won't run into" list. The "How it works" 1-2-3 tells me what happens after I tap. "Every business, person and number shown here is a made-up sample" at the bottom: honest, I respect it. Kills it: the owner quotes are gone, so there's zero proof anyone uses this. On the CRM page, half the FAQ answers are "We go over that on your demo call": card rate, payout timing, setup time, bringing my customers over, even who owns my data. The current pricing page already answers the data question in one sentence. Why hide it here?

**4. Unanswered.** Card processing rate and when the money hits my bank. How I get off Shopmonkey and how long it takes. Parts ordering, labor guide, VIN or plate decode, canned jobs, inspections. Does the AI receptionist quote prices or just book? Can texts go out in Spanish? Half my Garland customers would rather read it in Spanish. Who exactly calls me back.

**5. Try the CRM?** Yes, leaning yes. Price is clear, no contract, and they showed me the approval screen. I'd still probably call the number before filling a form.

**6. Favorite / least favorite.** Favorite: the car owner estimate with Approve / Decline per job and the $615 total. Least favorite: "we go over that on your demo call" six times.

## Concept B (dark purple, techy)

**1. First 10 seconds on the phone.** Same words as A, so I get it and I see the price. But the dark purple grid looks like a crypto app. On desktop the whole first screen is a huge headline on an empty dark background, no picture of the product. I'd keep scrolling, a little less sure who this is for.

**2. Built for a business like mine?** The home page walks a day in five steps (a call comes in, it's booked, the estimate goes out, they pay, you ask for the review). That's a nice way to explain it, but every screen is the HVAC company. The auto CRM page is the best one for reading screens on a phone: one per row, big. I could actually read the estimate: 2018 BMW 330i, front pads and rotors $420 with labor and parts split out, rear pads $195, brake fluid flush declined, "You are approving $615.00." If I showed that to my advisors they'd get it in five seconds. The caption bar sits over the bottom of each screen and cuts off part of it.

**3. Trust.** Same pricing clarity, same missing testimonials, same "made-up sample" honesty. No Pricing link in the top menu, so if I want price I have to scroll the home page.

**4. Unanswered.** Same list as A.

**5. Try the CRM?** Maybe. The auto page sold me more than the look did.

**6. Favorite / least favorite.** Favorite: big readable screens on the auto page. Least favorite: looks like a tech startup, not a company that knows a lift from a jack.

**1. First 10 seconds on the phone.** Phone number at the very top of the page, "Based in DFW, Texas," before anything else. I like that. Then the same headline, Try the CRM and the check marks. The price is just at the bottom of the first screen. I'd keep scrolling.

**2. Built for a business like mine?** It feels the most like a real local company and the least like software. The home page lists all twelve industries one after another with screenshots, which is a lot of scrolling on a phone to reach mine (Auto Repair is third, so not terrible). The auto page is titled "A day at Apex Auto Werks" and has the same screens as A and B, plus "Next: a day at Kestrel Lane Detail Co." But the auto page never says the price. I had to go back to find $99.

**3. Trust.** Builds it: the FAQ answers are all open, Q and A, no tapping. That's how I like it. Kills it: when the answers are open, you see even more clearly how many say "we go over that on your demo call." Card rate is one of them. No testimonials.

**4. Unanswered.** Same list. Plus the price on my industry page.

**5. Try the CRM?** Maybe.

**6. Favorite / least favorite.** Favorite: phone number and "Based in DFW" in the top bar, and open FAQ answers. Least favorite: no price on the auto page.

## My ranking

1. **Concept A.** Clearest on the phone, price and terms on every page including mine, the home page picker gets me to auto repair in one tap, Pricing is in the menu.
2. **Concept C.** Feels local and honest, open FAQ, but no price on my page and a long industry list.
3. **Concept B.** Best auto page on a phone, but the look says tech startup.
4. **Current site.** Best auto copy and the only one with testimonials, but the luxury look, the cost table and the stat boxes feel like marketing, and it never shows the customer approving a repair.

## The 8 changes I'd make to Concept A

1. **Lead the auto page with the car owner's estimate, full width on the phone.** On `industries/auto-repair-crm.html`, move "Car owner · Estimate" (Approve / Decline each job, photos, "You are approving $615.00") to first, right under the headline, one screen per row at phone width so the buttons are readable. Put the same screen on the home page "Estimates by text" card when Auto Repair is picked. That screen is the whole sale for a shop owner. Right now it's fifth and the size of a stamp. *(Steal from B: one big screen per row on phone.)*

2. **Replace "we go over that on your demo call" with numbers.** In the CRM page FAQ: card rate, when payouts land, typical setup time in days, how many minutes and texts "normal use" covers and what overage costs, and "Your data is yours and you can export it" (the current pricing page already says this; say it here too). Every dodge reads like there's a catch. *(Steal from current: the "your customers are always yours, they go with you if you leave" line.)*

3. **Add a "Switching from Shopmonkey, Tekmetric or Mitchell 1" section to the auto page.** Say what comes over (customers, vehicles, repair history, open ROs, price book), who does the move, how long, and whether I can run both for a week. The current site already has "Switching from Hibu/Thryv." Do the same for shop software, because that's what I'd be leaving.

4. **An honest "what's in it for a repair shop" checklist.** On the auto CRM page, a plain yes / not yet list: parts ordering (PartsTech, Worldpac), labor guide, VIN or plate decode, canned jobs, digital inspection with photos, tire and fleet accounts, QuickBooks. A "not yet" I can live with. Finding out on the demo call I can't. This is where I decide if it can replace my shop software or just sit next to it.

5. **Bring back proof, and get one from a repair shop.** A has no testimonials at all. Put two or three real owner quotes on the home page and one on the auto page, with the shop name and city. If there's no repair shop customer yet, say so and offer to put me on the phone with a current customer. *(Steal from current: the owner quote section with business names and "On the Signature plan.")*

6. **Make the sample shop look like a general repair shop.** Apex is a BMW performance shop, and the estimate is a 330i. Most independents in DFW are Camrys, F-150s and fleet vans. Either make the auto repair sample a general repair shop or add one line that says the site and CRM get set up for whatever you work on. Also answer whether estimate texts and the website can be in Spanish.

7. **Say who I'll talk to.** In "How it works" step 2 ("We call you"), put a name, a face, "in DFW," the hours, and how fast they call back. After signup, how do I get help: call, text, response time. I've been burned by support that's a ticket form. *(Steal from C: the top bar with "Based in DFW, Texas" and the phone number on every page.)*

8. **Tell me what the AI receptionist will and won't say.** On the auto page, under "Advisor · Stranded driver," show one real call flow: does it quote an oil change price, does it check bay availability, what happens when it doesn't know (transfers to an advisor? takes a message?). I won't let a robot promise my customers a price I didn't set. One honest example builds more trust than "answers every call."

**Also worth stealing:** from C, open FAQ answers instead of accordions. From current, the declined-work follow-up story ("He wasn't ready to spend the money that day... Nobody followed up") as a short block on the auto page, without the stat boxes. Leave behind the $4,520-a-month cost table. It's the one thing that made me trust the current site less.

## Would I tap Try the CRM?

On Concept A: **yes, leaning yes.** $99 a month for my whole crew, month to month, and they showed me the approval screen I actually want. I'd probably call the number first to ask about the card rate and moving off Shopmonkey, because the site won't tell me.
