The call you miss after a storm is the job that goes to the next crew

When a storm rolls through, the phone does not ring once, it rings all at once. Every homeowner on the block with a limb on the roof, a trunk across the driveway, or a tree leaning on the power line is calling at the same moment, and they are all calling more than one company. A tree on a house is not something anyone leaves a voicemail about and then waits on. They call down the list until a person picks up, and the crew that answers is the crew that gets the work, often the whole street of it.

But when the storm hits, you are already out on a job with a saw running, tied off in a canopy, or feeding the chipper, and nobody near the truck can hear the phone over the noise. So the surge you waited all season for rolls to voicemail, and the emergency removals, the ones that pay the most and cannot wait, go to whoever happened to be standing next to their phone. An AI receptionist answers every one of those calls on the first ring, stays steady with a rattled homeowner, finds out whether a tree is on the house or just down in the yard, gets the address, and either books it or flags it to you as a real emergency on the spot.

Built around how tree services actually get calls

Your work does not let you answer the phone. A ground crew running saws and a chipper cannot hear it, a climber forty feet up is not going to take a call, and the estimate you are walking is a conversation you cannot interrupt every time a new lead rings in. Meanwhile the calls range from a genuine emergency to a homeowner who just wants a price on taking down a dead oak someday, and a voicemail box cannot tell those two apart or tell the panicked one that help is coming.

  • Answers day and night, including the storm-night emergency and the weekend limb on a roof that are your best-paying calls.
  • Triages the way you would: is a tree on a structure or a vehicle, is it near a power line, is anyone in danger, or is this a routine quote for a removal, a trim, or stump grinding that can wait.
  • Gets the address and the details, books the estimates and routine work on your calendar, and texts you the true emergencies at once so you decide whether to pull a crew off what they are doing.
  • Treats a repeat customer or a referral differently from a first-time caller, so the people who already trust you never hit a voicemail.

The math is one storm removal

Catching even a handful of these calls pays for the whole thing. One emergency removal after a storm, the kind of job that has you there the same day with a bucket truck and a full crew, is often worth more than the system costs for months, and that storm work is usually your highest-margin ticket of the year. Every estimate and routine trim it captures after that first save is on top. The point is to stop handing the surge you count on to the outfit that simply answered while your saws were running.

And the surge is not spread evenly across the year. It lands in a few days after each big blow, exactly when every crew in town is slammed and homeowners are anxious and ready to hire the first company that shows up prepared. Being the one that answers in that window, instead of the one calling people back two days later, is the difference between a booked week and a quiet one.

You own the number, the calls, and the customer list

It answers on the number your trucks already carry, or a fresh one put in your name, never ours. Every caller, every address, and every job detail is yours and exportable any time, so the customer list you build stays an asset you own instead of something you rent back month to month, and it is there for you whether you stay with us for years or leave tomorrow. No multi-year contract sits on top of your data. The AI receptionist is one piece of the Top Shelf platform, and it hands every call it captures straight to the same CRM that follows up, so an estimate you quoted or a seasonal trimming reminder never slips through the cracks between jobs. It earns its keep the first stormy weekend, and it keeps earning it on the quiet days by catching the estimate calls that used to roll to voicemail while you were up in a tree.

How it plays out

A midnight tree through the roof, booked while you sleep

It is the middle of the night in a bad windstorm and a homeowner wakes to a branch punched through the bedroom ceiling. They search for a tree service and call the first three they find. Two go to voicemail. Yours answers, stays calm, confirms nobody is hurt and the family is out of that room, gets the address, and books your crew for first light while flagging it to your phone as urgent. You wake up to the job already on your calendar with the address, the problem, and the customer's number attached, instead of hearing about it after they hired someone else.

Common Questions

01

Does it work with my current phone number?

Yes. It can answer on your existing business number, or set up a new one registered in your name. Either way the number and every call that comes through it belong to you and go with you if you ever leave.

02

Can it tell a real emergency from a routine estimate?

Yes, that is the point. It asks the questions you would, is a tree on a house or a vehicle, is it near a power line, is anyone in danger, and treats that as urgent while booking a someday removal, a trim, or a stump grind as routine. You set what counts as a drop-everything emergency.

03

Will it actually book the job on my calendar?

Yes. Estimates and routine work land straight on your calendar based on your availability, and you get a text with the details right away. For a true emergency it can book the first open slot and alert you at once so you decide whether to pull a crew off what they are on.

04

Is it going to sound like a robot to a panicked homeowner?

It answers naturally and calmly, and it is upfront instead of pretending to be a person. Someone staring at a tree on their roof mostly needs to know a real crew is handling it, and a steady voice that captures the details beats a voicemail box every time. You can hear it handle a live call before you decide.
