Where the money goes

Four Gaps Costing Retail & Local Shops Every Week

Every one of these is a problem we can actually fix, and every fix links to how we do it. Follow the thread as far as you want.

The phone rings while you're ringing someone up

You're wrapping a gift for one customer and helping another find a card when the phone rings. Someone wants to know if you carry a certain candle and whether you're open till six. It rings out. She drives to the big-box store instead.

When you're the only one behind the counter, the customer in front of you comes first, and the phone waits. Most callers don't leave a message. They just call the next place.

How Top Shelf fixes it: The AI receptionist answers every call in your shop’s name while you help the customer in front of you. If a call still gets missed, missed-call text-back texts the caller right away. More on the AI receptionist →

You get: The phone answered while you take care of the people in the store.

They browse with you, then buy on their phone

A customer spends fifteen minutes with you talking through two candles, snaps a photo of the label, thanks you warmly, and leaves empty-handed. That night the same candle shows up on her doorstep from an online seller.

Showrooming and webrooming: shoppers use your store to see, touch, and get advice, but the actual purchase happens online where a price or a link is one tap away. When you have no online presence of your own, that sale simply gaps to a marketplace. Every 'let me think about it' that turns into an online order is full-margin revenue you did the work to earn and then handed to a competitor who never met the customer.

81%of retail shoppers do online research before buying, and over half prefer to see a product in-store before purchasing it online. helplama.com

How Top Shelf fixes it: A professional website for $499 one time gives your shop a real home online, with your photos, your hours and what you carry. The AI chatbot on your site answers questions after you close, and the contact form sends every question straight into the CRM. More on websites →

You get: A website that looks like your shop, and answers when they look you up after they leave.

When someone searches what you sell, a chain shows up instead of you

A shopper two blocks away types 'gift shop near me' into her phone. Your unclaimed, half-empty Google listing sits below three big-box stores, so she drives right past your window to one of them.

If your website is missing, dated or hard for Google to read, you’re invisible at the exact moment a ready-to-buy neighbor is looking for what you carry. These aren’t tire-kickers. Near-me searchers are minutes from buying, and every one that lands on a competitor is a walk-in you should have had.

76%of people who run a 'near me' search visit a business within 24 hours, and 28% of local searches end in a purchase. biziq.com

How Top Shelf fixes it: We build your website so Google can read it: clear titles, headings and descriptions for what you sell and where you are. After each sale, the CRM can text a review request so your reviews stay fresh. More on websites →

You get: A site that says plainly what you sell and where, and a steady stream of review requests.

First-time buyers leave and you have no way to bring them back

A tourist buys eighty dollars of your candles, gushes about the shop, and walks out the door forever. You have no name, no email, no phone number, no way to ever reach her again.

No captured contacts means every sale is a stranger you have to win from scratch. Your best asset, the people who already love the place, walks out unlogged. It costs far more to land a new customer than to bring a happy one back for a second and third visit.

How Top Shelf fixes it: Every customer who calls, books or pays by card lands in the CRM with their name and number. Add notes on what they like, set a follow-up reminder, and text them from the app when that thing they asked about comes in. More on the CRM →

You get: A customer list that’s yours, and a real reason to reach out to each one.
