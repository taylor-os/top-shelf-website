The quotes you already sent are the jobs you are losing

Most electricians do not have a lead problem. They have a follow-up problem. You quote a panel upgrade, the homeowner says they need to think it over, and you never hear back, not because the price was wrong but because a panel upgrade is a considered purchase people sit on for weeks, and nobody stayed in touch while they did. The quote was never dead. It just needed a check-in every few days, and that is the thing there is never time for between service calls.

A CRM catches every quote and lead and works it on a schedule you set, with texts and emails that go out on time whether or not you remember. The homeowner who is still deciding on that upgrade gets a friendly nudge, still sees your name, and still calls you when they are ready instead of the electrician who happened to follow up.

Your past customers are the business

The homeowner you did a repair for last year is the cheapest job you will ever get. They already trust you inside their home, and a repeat call or a referral costs you nothing to win. But you cannot personally remember to touch hundreds of past customers on the right cadence, so most of that goodwill sits unused and slowly forgets who you are.

  • Every customer, note, and past job lives in one place instead of your head, your truck, and a stack of invoices.
  • Maintenance reminders, safety-check nudges, and seasonal touches go out on schedule, so past customers hear from you without you setting a single reminder.
  • You see who is worth a call, so the ten minutes you have goes to the homeowner who is actually close to saying yes.

Quote follow-up that happens without you

A panel upgrade, a rewire, a generator or EV charger install, these are the jobs people compare and delay on, and they are exactly the jobs a steady follow-up wins. When you send a quote, the CRM starts a sequence: a check-in the next day, a helpful answer to the usual permit and timeline questions a few days later, a last nudge before it goes cold. You approve the messages once, and every quote you send gets worked the same way, whether you sent one this week or a dozen.

You own the customer list, and it plugs into everything

Every customer and every note is yours and exportable any time. Your list is an asset you own, not something you rent access to. The CRM is one piece of the Top Shelf platform and connects to the AI receptionist, so a call it captures lands in your list and gets followed up on automatically, and to online booking, so a scheduled install is logged against the right customer instead of a sticky note on the dash.

How it plays out

The panel-upgrade quote you forgot about gets approved

You quote a homeowner for a panel upgrade in the spring. They say they need to talk it over, and you would normally lose track of them by summer. Instead the CRM keeps a light touch going, a quick check-in, a note answering the permit question they had, all sent for you. In July the homeowner texts that they are ready to move forward, and they text you, because yours is the name that stayed in front of them. You did not lift a finger to keep that quote alive.
