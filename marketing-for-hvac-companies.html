HVAC demand is seasonal and spiky, and visibility has to come first

Your phone does not ring evenly all year. It is quiet in the shoulder seasons and then explodes on the first 100-degree afternoon and the first hard freeze, when everyone who ignored a struggling system suddenly needs you on the same day. The trouble is that visibility is not a switch you flip when the heat wave hits. The company that shows up first in that moment is the one that was already ranking, already posting, already in front of people for weeks before demand arrived. Waiting until you are slow to start marketing means you are invisible exactly when it counts most.

Your Google Business Profile is the new storefront

When a homeowner searches for HVAC repair near them, your Google Business Profile and the map pack are often the first thing they see, before your website and before a directory. A profile that sits untouched for months looks abandoned next to one with recent photos, seasonal posts, and a steady stream of fresh reviews. Keeping it active, with real posts about tune-up season, financing on replacements, and the work you are doing, is a quiet advertisement running in the exact moment someone is looking for a company like yours.

Fill the slow seasons instead of only surviving the busy ones

The money in HVAC is not only in the summer emergencies. It is in the tune-ups booked in spring and fall, the shoulder seasons most companies leave on the table. Marketing that pushes pre-season maintenance before the heat and before the cold smooths out the valleys, keeps your techs busy when they would otherwise be idle, and turns a one-time repair customer into a maintenance-plan member who calls you first for years. It is steadier, cheaper business than fighting for the same emergency calls everyone else is chasing in July.

Your past customers are your cheapest demand

The homeowners you have already served are the easiest business you will ever get and the easiest to lose touch with. A steady, low-effort presence, a seasonal tune-up reminder, a note about a financing offer on replacements, a helpful tip before the cold sets in, keeps you top of mind so the repeat call and the referral come to you instead of whoever they saw most recently. This is the public-facing side of your business. The private follow-up to the customers already in your database is the CRM.

How it plays out

The pre-season post that fills your spring

Before summer, your Google profile runs steady posts about getting an AC tune-up done before the heat arrives. A homeowner who has been meaning to deal with their aging system sees one while searching, books a tune-up in April instead of waiting for a July breakdown, and signs up for a maintenance plan on the visit. Months later, when their system finally needs replacing, they call you without shopping around, because you are the company that has been in front of them all along. You filled a slow month and earned a plan member and a future install from one post.
