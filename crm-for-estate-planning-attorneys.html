The client who keeps meaning to finish their plan is the one who never does

Most estate planning firms do not have a call problem. They have a follow-through problem, and it is a particular kind. Someone comes in for a consultation, agrees they need a will or a trust, takes the folder home to gather account numbers and think about who they want as guardian and executor, and then life closes back over the whole thing. There is no deadline forcing it, nobody is chasing them, and the task is unpleasant enough that it slides to the bottom of the list. Months pass. They were never a bad prospect. They simply needed a reason to pick it back up, and that is the nudge there is never time to send between drafting and signings.

A CRM keeps every consultation that has not turned into a finished plan in front of you and follows up for you, on a schedule you set, with messages that sound like your firm and never pressure. It is not a sales chase; it is a patient reminder that the plan still matters and that you are ready whenever they are. The person who took the folder home and stalled hears from you again at the right moment, instead of letting another year go by, or finally getting the plan done somewhere else after a friend pushed them into it.

A will written years ago quietly stops working

Here is the part of estate planning most firms leave on the table. A plan is not finished the day it is signed; it goes stale. The couple named a guardian who has since moved across the country. A named executor has passed away. There is a new child or grandchild the plan never mentions, a divorce or a remarriage, a house bought, a business sold, a move to a new state whose laws work differently. The tax rules shift under everyone every few years. A plan nobody has revisited is the plan that fails at the exact moment a family is counting on it, and the client almost never thinks to come back on their own. A steady reminder to review is worth real money to your firm and real peace of mind to them.

  • Every client, plan, and note lives in one place, so you can see at a glance whose documents are aging and who has not been in for a review in years.
  • Automatic review reminders go out on the cadence you set, so a client hears from you when it is time to dust off the plan, and the update work comes back to your firm instead of never happening at all.
  • When a client mentions a life change, a birth, a death, a move, a marriage, you can flag it so the plan gets revisited before it quietly falls out of date.
  • The people whose plans you refresh stay your clients, and their families stay close for the administration work that comes later.

Your pipeline is advisors, CPAs, and the families you have already helped

More than almost any other practice, estate planning runs on professional referral relationships. Financial advisors, CPAs, insurance agents, and wealth managers each sit on a book of clients who all need a will or a trust and who trust their advisor to point them to a lawyer. One good relationship can send a steady stream of work for years, and it runs both ways, since you send clients back to them too. But those relationships go cold the moment they are neglected, and a busy attorney cannot keep a mental list of who sent whom or who is due for a thank-you. A CRM keeps a light, organized touch on every referral source, a note when they send someone your way, a check-in so you stay top of mind, a heads-up when a shared client's plan is done, so you are the name that comes up the next time an advisor's client asks who to call. The same steady touch keeps past clients close, and past clients are how the administration and probate work, the matters that arrive when someone passes, come back to the firm the family already trusts.

One list, wired to your calls and every plan you have written

Every client and every note belongs to you and can be exported whenever you like, never sealed inside a tool you lease by the month. The CRM is one piece of the Top Shelf platform: the consultations your answering service books flow into it and the follow-up begins on its own, and each meeting and next step hangs on the right person so nothing lives in two systems or falls out of both. Your estate planning drafting and practice-management software still holds the documents themselves, the matter files, and the legal deadlines. The CRM works the human side of a relationship that is meant to last decades: the people who have not finished their plan, the clients whose documents are quietly going out of date, and the advisors and families who send you the next matter. It carries the relationship while your attorneys carry the law and make every legal decision, and it never promises a client a thing about how their estate will be handled or taxed.

How it plays out

The trust that gets reviewed before it fails

A couple set up a living trust with you six years ago, and you have not spoken since. Normally that is where the relationship quietly ends, until something goes wrong and a family discovers the plan no longer fits. Instead the CRM sends a gentle review reminder on the cadence you set, and it lands just as the couple has welcomed a second grandchild and watched the relative they named as guardian move out of state. They come back in, update the plan while it can still be done calmly, and leave feeling looked after rather than sold to. Years later, when one of them passes, there is no question which firm the family calls to handle it. Nobody on your team had to keep the date in their head.

Questions Estate Planning Attorneys Ask

Common Questions

01

Does it import my existing clients and matters?

Yes. Your current clients, past consultations, and matter notes come in and live in one place, all of it yours and exportable. The point is to put the relationships your firm has already built to work instead of letting them sit in a phone and an inbox.

02

Will it follow up on someone who came in but never finished their plan?

Yes, on the schedule you approve. A consultation that has not turned into a signed plan gets a gentle, no-pressure check-in later and another after that, sent for you, so a person who took the folder home and stalled keeps hearing that you are ready whenever they are. You can step in and reach anyone yourself any time.

03

Can it remind past clients to review and update their will or trust?

Yes, and for an estate practice that is often the biggest win. You set the cadence, and it reminds clients when their documents are aging or when a life change means the plan should be revisited, so the update work comes back to you and, just as important, the plan actually still works when a family needs it.

04

Can it help me stay in front of the financial advisors and CPAs who refer me?

Yes. It keeps an organized, light touch on your referral sources, a thank-you when they send someone, a check-in so you stay top of mind, so those relationships stay warm instead of fading. In estate planning that professional network is often the single largest source of new matters, and it is exactly what tends to get neglected when everyone is busy.

05

How is this different from my estate planning drafting and practice-management software?

It sits alongside it. That software holds the documents, the matter files, and the legal deadlines. This runs everything around them: the consultations you have not signed, the past clients whose plans are going stale, and the advisors and families who refer you. The two do different jobs, and setup is included so it is working within days.
