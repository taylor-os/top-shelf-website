Looking for a Clio Alternative for Your Law Firm?

Most firms weighing a Clio alternative are really asking one of two different questions. Clio is legal practice management software, built to help a firm run its work: matters and cases, calendars and deadlines, time tracking, billing, documents, and intake forms. Top Shelf is a done-for-you platform and agency built to help fill a practice: getting the firm found, answering and booking the new-client call, and following up on every lead. If your gap is managing cases, that is Clio's job. If your gap is capturing new clients and getting found, that is ours. A number of firms use both.

What Clio does well

Clio is a well-known legal practice management platform, and firms rely on it for good reason. Its own materials describe a single place to run the day-to-day work of a law firm: opening and organizing matters, tracking calendars and deadlines, logging billable time, generating invoices, storing case documents, and taking in new client details through intake forms. It is used across solo practices, growing firms, and larger legal teams, by the attorneys, paralegals, and staff who work in those files every day. If your bottleneck is keeping open matters organized and getting bills out the door, that is the kind of work Clio is built for.

That is a real strength, and it helps to be precise about what it means. Practice management software is a tool your own team signs into and operates, and it keeps the work you already have organized and billed. That is a different thing from a service that answers your new-client call at nine at night, or a team that keeps your website, reviews, and lead follow-up running for you while the lawyers practice law. Both matter. They are just not the same job, and most firms feel the second gap long before the software can close it.

Where Top Shelf is different

Top Shelf is not another piece of software for your team to learn and run. It is a done-for-you platform and agency, managed for the owner, on one monthly bill. Instead of the back office, it works on the front of the firm: the website and local search presence that helps prospective clients find you, an AI receptionist that answers the phone around the clock and books the intake call onto your calendar, a CRM that follows up on every lead so none go cold, and review generation that builds the reputation new clients check first.

The AI receptionist is deliberately narrow about what it does. It handles intake and booking only. It greets the caller, gathers basic contact and matter-type details, answers routine questions about the firm, and schedules the consultation. It does not give legal advice, take on a case, or make any judgment that belongs to a lawyer. When a call needs a real attorney, it routes the caller to you. The point is simple: a new-client call that used to reach voicemail now reaches a booked consultation, and you own the number, the leads, and the data behind all of it.

Do you need one, the other, or both?

Here is the honest way to choose. If your real problem is managing open matters, tracking time, sending bills, or keeping case documents and deadlines in order, that is practice-management territory, and Top Shelf does not replace it. If your real problem is that new-client calls slip to voicemail, leads never get a second touch, or people in your area cannot easily find and choose your firm, that is the gap Top Shelf is built to fill, and a case-management tool will not close it on its own. Plenty of firms end up running both: one system to manage the work, and one done-for-you service to bring it in. The way to know which you need is to look at where clients are actually slipping away, and a free audit shows you that whether you work with us or not.
