CRM for House Cleaning Companies

A CRM for house cleaning companies keeps every client, quote, and one-time clean in one place and follows up for you, so the move-out clean you did once becomes a recurring plan and the weekly client who went quiet gets caught before they churn. Your client list quietly becomes recurring revenue.

The one-time cleans you already did are the recurring clients you are losing

Most cleaning companies do not have a lead problem. They have a follow-up problem. You do a great deep clean or a move-out, the client is thrilled, and then nobody ever asks them to go on a schedule. A one-time clean is a one-time payment unless someone follows up and offers weekly or biweekly service while the memory of a spotless house is still fresh. That is the single most valuable conversation in the business, and it is the one there is never time for between crews and quotes.

A CRM keeps every one-time clean and open quote in front of you and follows up on a schedule you set, with texts and emails that go out on time whether or not anyone at the office remembers. The client who loved the deep clean gets asked to make it regular, the quote someone is sitting on gets a nudge, and the recurring plan closes instead of the job ending at one visit.

Every recurring client is worth more the longer they stay

The money in cleaning is not in any single job, it is in the client who stays on the schedule for years. That client already trusts your crew with a key and an alarm code, already has a card on file, and is the cheapest revenue you will ever have. But you cannot hold every home, every preference, and every recurring date in your head, so details get lost and clients drift when the service stops feeling personal.

  • Every client, address, gate or alarm code, product preference, pet note, and recurring schedule lives in one place instead of scattered across memory and a stack of paper.
  • Recurring cleans stay on schedule, and clients who are due, or overdue, surface automatically instead of slipping off the calendar.
  • You can see who has not booked in a while and reach the right client with the right offer before they drift to another service.

The recurring clients slipping away are still yours to keep

A recurring client rarely calls to quit. They just skip a week, then another, and quietly stop rebooking, and you do not notice until the route has a hole in it. That silent churn is the most expensive thing in a cleaning business, because replacing a long-standing client costs far more than keeping one. A CRM flags the client who has gone quiet or pushed off two cleans in a row, so you can reach out with a check-in or a small win-back offer while there is still a client to keep, instead of finding the gap after they are gone.

You own the list, and it works with the rest of the system

Every client and every note is yours and exportable any time, not locked inside software you rent. The CRM is one piece of the Top Shelf platform and connects to the AI receptionist, so a call it answers lands in your database and gets followed up on automatically, and to online booking, so a recurring slot or a one-time clean is logged against the right client with their full history and preferences already attached. Nothing you have earned goes cold.

How it plays out

The deep clean that becomes a standing Thursday

A new client books a one-time deep clean before hosting family for the holidays, and your crew leaves the house spotless. Normally that is the end of it. Instead the CRM sends a warm check-in two days later, while the house still feels perfect, asking if they would like to keep it that way every other week, and it sounds like you wrote it. The client says yes, and a one-time job turns into a standing biweekly Thursday slot that pays for years. You never sat down to make the ask.
