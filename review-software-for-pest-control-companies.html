People read your reviews before they let you inside

Hiring an exterminator is not like hiring most trades. The customer is about to let someone into the bedroom to deal with bed bugs, or into the kitchen over a roach problem they are embarrassed to admit, and they size you up the only way a stranger can, by reading what other people in the same spot said afterward. Before they ever dial, they scroll your reviews looking for someone who was discreet, thorough, and did not make them feel judged. A profile with a few stale reviews reads as a gamble, and the nervous homeowner quietly picks the company whose recent reviews put them at ease.

That is rarely a sign your work is worse. Satisfied customers just move on once the problem is handled and never think to post, and you feel awkward bringing it up while you are loading the sprayer back onto the truck. Review software closes that gap for you, so the trust you keep earning in person finally shows up online where the next worried customer is deciding.

The review only pest control earns: not one since

There is a kind of review pest control earns that almost no other trade does: the customer who writes that they have not seen a single roach since your visit, or that the ants marching across the counter every summer are finally gone for good. That line, not one since, is the most persuasive proof a nervous searcher can find, because it answers the one thing they actually care about, whether the treatment holds. Reviews that name the exact pest go even further. Someone typing bed bug treatment into Google and finding a review that says you cleared a bed bug problem two doors down is far likelier to call than they would be off a generic star rating. The software nudges customers toward describing what you handled, so your profile fills with the specific wins that pull in the next person facing the same thing.

Ask when the plan proves itself, not just after the first spray

Timing is trickier here than in a one-and-done trade. A first treatment might knock down what the customer can see, but they are not truly at ease until a week or two later when nothing has returned, and a request sent too early misses that feeling entirely. The software can hold the ask until the follow-up visit or the point where the pest is confirmed gone, then send it by text and email with a one-tap link to your profile. Your recurring plan members are the real prize here: someone who has stayed on quarterly service for a year without a hitch writes the review that sells the plan itself, and the software can prompt them at a natural moment instead of only chasing one-time jobs.

Answer the hard ones with care, and let it compound

Replies matter more when the subject is sensitive. A customer who worked up the nerve to review a bed bug job deserves a warm, discreet thank-you that never repeats the embarrassing details, and a rare unhappy review, a missed window or a treatment that needed a second round, deserves a composed public answer that shows the next reader how you make it right. The software flags each new review as it posts so nothing sits ignored. Every genuine review you gather strengthens your Google Business Profile, which is what pushes you up the local map when someone nearby searches for an exterminator, which brings the calls that produce the next round of reviews. That loop belongs to you, attached to your own profile rather than a directory, and the requests fire on their own from a job you close out in your CRM.

How it plays out

A bed bug review that becomes next month's booking

You finish a bed bug job for a family on a Monday and set the standard follow-up. Two weeks later, once they confirm nothing has returned, an automatic message invites them to share how it went, and they leave a relieved review that mentions how carefully you treated the bedrooms and that there has not been a bite since. A month on, someone three streets away wakes up to bites of their own, looks for help, and finds that review sitting at the top of your profile. It is the exact reassurance they needed, so they call you and no one else.

Common Questions

01

Will people really review something as private as bed bugs or roaches?

More often than you would think. A customer who was mortified and then genuinely helped is usually glad to reassure their neighbors, and the ask makes it painless. You can also steer the wording so they praise your work without spelling out the details, and your discreet reply signals that you keep sensitive jobs quiet.

02

Some of my treatments need a follow-up. When does it ask?

You pick the trigger. Rather than firing the second you drive off, it can wait for the follow-up visit or the point where the pest is confirmed gone, which is when the customer actually feels the result and writes the strongest review. The timing and message are yours to set.

03

Can it collect reviews from my recurring plan members too?

Yes, and they are often your best. A member who has stayed on quarterly service without an issue for a year writes the review that convinces the next homeowner to sign a plan rather than book a single spray. The software can prompt plan members at a natural moment, not just chase one-off jobs.

04

Is automating the requests within Google's rules?

Yes. Asking everyone you served for an honest review is exactly what Google wants. The lines you cannot cross are gating, quietly skipping the customers who might complain, and buying reviews outright, and the software does neither. It invites every real customer and makes leaving feedback effortless.

05

How fast is it working, and which sites does it cover?

It centers on Google, where an anxious searcher looks first and where your local ranking is decided, and it can point past customers to your other profiles too.
