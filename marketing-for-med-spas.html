Marketing for Med Spas

Med spa marketing keeps you visible where new clients actually decide, on Instagram and TikTok where they scroll before-and-afters at night, and in the Google map pack when they search for a spa nearby, so the person weighing a first treatment finds you, trusts you, and books a consult instead of a competitor.

Aesthetic demand is created on a screen, after hours

People do not decide to get Botox or filler at a desk during business hours. The idea forms late at night, scrolling Instagram and TikTok, watching before-and-afters and someone walk honestly through what a treatment was actually like. By the time they search for a spa nearby, they have half-decided already, and they are choosing among the few names they recognize and trust. If you are not part of that scroll, and not visible when they finally search, you are not in the running, no matter how skilled your injector is.

Marketing for a med spa is about being present and trusted in those two places, the feed where the interest starts and the search where the decision lands, so yours is the name that already feels familiar the moment someone is ready to book.

Your feed is the storefront, and your results are the proof

No one books an aesthetic treatment on words alone. They want to see real results on real faces, and a steady feed of before-and-afters you have consent to post, treatment clips, and the people behind the spa is what turns a curious scroller into a booked consult. A page that has gone quiet for two months reads as a spa that closed or stopped caring, sitting right next to one posting current work. Keeping your presence active, consistent, and honest, built around the treatments you actually want more of, is a quiet advertisement running exactly where new clients are looking.

The content that actually converts is not a glossy ad. It is a nurse answering the questions that keep a first-timer on the fence, does it hurt, how long does it last, will it look overdone, next to real results and the faces behind the treatments. That is what eases the nerves standing between someone and a first appointment, and it is the kind of thing a busy front desk never finds time to post on a schedule.

When they finally search, the map pack decides

Once someone is ready, they search for a med spa near them or a treatment and their town, and the first thing they see is the map pack, the three local listings with the star ratings, sitting above the websites and the ads. A Google Business Profile with recent photos, current hours, your real menu, and a steady flow of reviews wins that glance over a thin, neglected listing every time. Reviews carry more weight here than in almost any business, because a new client is trusting you with her face and looking for proof that other people did the same and were glad. Keeping that profile active and those reviews fresh is what puts you in front of the ready-to-book search.

Stay in front, and let good visits feed the next client

Aesthetic demand keeps its own calendar, wedding and event season, the run-up to holidays and vacations, the new-year reset, and being visible right before each wave beats scrambling once it arrives. A steady presence, current posts, fresh reviews, a profile that looks tended, keeps you top of mind for the next new client and reminds past ones that you are still the place to come back to. This is the public-facing side of the business; the private follow-up to the clients already in your database is the CRM, and the reviews all of this leans on are gathered automatically by the same Top Shelf platform each time a visit is marked complete.

How it plays out

A late-night scroll that becomes a morning consult

A woman is up late scrolling and lands on a lip-filler before-and-after from your spa, then a short clip of your injector walking through how the appointment goes. She follows you, and over the next week a couple more posts and a run of recent five-star reviews cross her feed. When she finally searches for a med spa in her town, yours is right there in the map pack with a full, active profile and the same reviews she already saw. She books a consult with the one name that already felt familiar and safe, not the listing she had never heard of.

Illustrative example, not a client.
