The quotes you already sent are the jobs you are losing

Most locksmiths do not have a lead problem. They have a follow-up problem. You quote a homeowner on rekeying the whole house, a business on new commercial locks, or a landlord on a master-key setup, they say they need to think it over or check with a partner, and then the next lockout call pulls you away and you never circle back. They collect another price, and the job goes to whoever followed up, not always the lowest bid. The quote was never dead. It just needed one more call or text a few days later, and that is the thing there is never time for between service calls.

A CRM keeps every open quote in front of you and follows up on a schedule you set, with texts and emails that go out on time whether or not you remember. The customer weighing two or three locksmiths hears from you again while the others go quiet, and the job comes back to you.

The commercial and property-manager account is the goldmine you already own

An emergency lockout might be a one-time customer, but the accounts that pay a locksmith for years are commercial. A property manager rekeys on every unit turnover, an office changes locks when someone leaves, a landlord runs dozens of doors, a growing business keeps adding access control, and all of it is repeat work that goes to the locksmith who stayed in touch and made it easy. The trouble is you meet these accounts on a single call and then lose track of them between the emergencies that fill your day.

  • Every account, contact, address, and the hardware you installed lives in one place, so when a property manager calls about a turnover you already know the building and the keyway.
  • Follow-ups go out on a schedule you set, a check-in after a big install, a note to a manager as a leasing season comes around, so the repeat work returns without you tracking it.
  • You can see which commercial accounts have gone quiet and reach the right contact before they try another locksmith.

The homeowner you rekeyed once is still your security relationship

A homeowner you helped after a move or a break-in is not disloyal when they call another locksmith. They just lost your card and never saved your number. A light, steady touch, a reminder that you also handle safes and smart locks, a quick check that the new deadbolts are working fine, keeps your name in their phone for the next lost key, the next rekey, the next upgrade. It costs almost nothing, and because you were the one trusted with their security once, you are the natural call the next time it matters.

You own the list, and it works with the rest of the system

Every customer and every note is yours and exportable any time, not locked inside software you rent. The CRM is one piece of the Top Shelf platform and connects to the answering service, so a call it answers lands in your database and gets followed up on automatically, and to online booking, so a scheduled job is logged against the right customer or account with its full history already attached. Nothing you have earned goes cold.

How it plays out

One lockout becomes a standing rekey account

A property manager calls you late on a Friday to let a tenant back into a unit. You handle it, and normally that is the end of it. Instead the job goes into your CRM against that manager and their building, and a couple of weeks later it sends a friendly check-in offering to be their go-to for unit-turnover rekeys. They had been calling whoever was free, but yours is the name in front of them now, so they start sending you every turnover. One after-hours lockout quietly turned into a standing account you never had to chase from memory.
