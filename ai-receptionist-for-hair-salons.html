The call you miss is the new client who books the salon that answered

A stylist with both hands in a client's hair cannot pick up the phone, and a new client calling to book a first appointment is not going to leave a voicemail and wait. They hang up and call the next salon, and the one after that, until someone answers. The work at the chair is the reason the phone rings out, and the new client, along with every visit they would have made all year, goes to whoever was free to answer.

An answering service picks up on the first ring, day or night, sounds warm instead of rushed, asks what the client wants done and roughly when, and either books the appointment or takes the details so nothing is lost. The new client is captured and scheduled instead of handed to the salon down the street that simply got to the phone first.

Most new clients find you at night, on their phone

A huge share of new-client interest never comes during salon hours at all. Someone sees a color transformation on Instagram at ten at night, taps through, and wants to book while the inspiration is fresh, or scrolling on a lunch break they fire off a quick message about extensions. If that message sits unanswered until tomorrow, the moment has passed and they have already booked somewhere that replied while they were still looking.

The answering service replies instantly to a text or a message, not just a call, and turns that late-night curiosity into a booked appointment on the spot. The client who messaged at eleven at night wakes up on your calendar instead of still shopping around.

Built around how a salon actually books

A booking is not one-size-fits-all, and a generic call center reading a script does not know a root touch-up from a full balayage, or that a first-time color client needs more chair time than a trim. The answering service handles it the way your front desk would on a good day.

  • Answers calls, texts, and messages 24/7, so the client who finds you at midnight and the one who calls on a Sunday both reach a real reply instead of a voicemail box.
  • Books the right service with the right stylist for the right amount of time, and knows a new color client is not a quick fifteen-minute appointment.
  • Treats a loyal regular differently from a first-time caller, so the clients who already trust you never feel like they are talking to a stranger.
  • Takes the details for anything it cannot book outright, a consultation or a color correction, and hands it straight to you to confirm.

You own the number, the calls, and the client list

This runs on your existing salon number, or a new one registered in your name, not ours. Every caller, every message, and every booking is yours and exportable any time, so the client list you are building is an asset you keep instead of something you rent back month to month, with no long contract holding your clients hostage. The answering service is one piece of the Top Shelf platform, and it hands every booking it captures to the same CRM that rebooks and follows up, so a new client turns into a regular instead of a one-time visit.

Does it work with my current salon phone number?

Yes. It can answer on your existing salon number, or set up a new one registered in your name. Either way the number and every call, text, and booking that comes through it belong to you and go with you if you ever leave.

02

Can it answer texts and Instagram messages, not just calls?

Yes, and that matters for a salon. A lot of new clients reach out by text or a message after seeing your work at night, and the answering service replies to those instantly, answers the first question, and books the appointment, so a late-night message does not sit until morning.

03

Will it actually book the appointment on the right stylist's calendar?

Yes. It books the right service with the right stylist for the right amount of time based on your calendar and rules, and you get the details right away. For anything that needs your judgment, a big color correction or a consultation, it takes the details and hands it to you to confirm.

Will it sound like a robot to a new client?

It answers naturally and warmly, and it is upfront rather than pretending to be a person. A new client mostly wants to know they can get in and that a real salon has them booked, and a friendly reply that captures the details beats a voicemail box every time.
