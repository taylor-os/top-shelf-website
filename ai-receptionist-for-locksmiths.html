The call you miss is the lockout that calls the next locksmith

A person locked out of their car in a parking lot, out of their house after dark, or a shop owner stuck on the sidewalk before opening is not going to leave a voicemail. They are stranded, and they call down the list until someone picks up, quotes them, and gives them a time. Locksmithing runs on that urgency more than almost any trade, because the customer cannot get into their car, their home, or their business until you arrive, and every minute they wait is a minute they are stuck outside. But your tech is halfway through picking a lock across town, cutting a key, or programming a fob with both hands busy, so the call rolls to voicemail and the job, along with the after-hours premium that comes with it, goes to whoever answered and named an arrival time.

A locksmith answering service picks up on the first ring, stays calm with a stranded caller, finds out whether it is a car, a house, or a business, gets the location, gives a realistic arrival window, and either books it or flags a true emergency straight to your phone. The job is captured and scheduled instead of lost to the shop that simply answered.

Built around how locksmiths actually get calls

Your calls do not keep office hours. Someone locks their keys in the car at eleven at night, a tenant is stuck outside an apartment on a weekend, a business owner snaps a key off in the lock before opening, and the calls that pay the most tend to come when every other shop is closed. A voicemail box cannot calm a stranded caller or gather what a job needs, and a generic call center reading a script does not know a car lockout from a mortise rekey, or a single deadbolt from a commercial master-key system.

  • Answers 24/7, including the late-night car lockout and the weekend house lockout that are your best-paying emergency jobs.
  • Triages like you would: car, home, or business, locked out or a broken key, one lock or a whole building, and how fast someone needs you there.
  • Gets the location and the details, gives a sensible arrival window, books routine work on your calendar, and texts you a true emergency at once so you decide whether to roll out now.
  • Handles a repeat commercial account or a property manager differently from a first-time caller, so the people who already trust you never hit a voicemail.

The math is one lockout, or one commercial account

You do not need to catch many calls for this to pay for itself. One late-night car lockout or one commercial rekey you would have lost while your hands were full is often worth more than the service costs for months, and after-hours emergency work is usually your highest-margin ticket of all. Better still, a first call answered well is how a property manager or a business becomes a repeat account instead of a one-time job. Everything it captures after that first save is on top. The point is to stop handing your best calls to the locksmith who simply picked up faster.

You own the number, the calls, and the customer list

This runs on your existing business number, or a new one registered in your name, not ours. Every caller, every address, and every job detail is yours and exportable any time, so your customer list, including the commercial accounts worth the most to you, is an asset you keep instead of something you rent back month to month. There is no long contract holding your data hostage. The locksmith answering service is one piece of the Top Shelf platform, and it hands every call it captures to the same CRM that follows up, so a quote or a repeat commercial account never slips.

How it plays out

An 11pm car lockout, booked with an ETA

It is eleven at night and someone is locked out of their car in a dark store parking lot, alone and rattled. They search for a locksmith and call the first three they see. Two go to voicemail. Yours answers, stays calm, confirms the car and the location, gives a twenty-minute arrival window, and books it while pinging your phone that it is urgent. Your tech was finishing a rekey across town and never had to break off to grab the phone. Questions Locksmiths Ask

Common Questions

01

Does it work with my current phone number?

Yes. It can answer on your existing business number, or set up a new one registered in your name. Either way the number and every call that comes through it belong to you and go with you if you ever leave.

02

Can it tell an emergency lockout from routine work?

Yes, that is the point. It asks what you would ask, car, home, or business, locked out or a broken key, one lock or a whole building, and treats a stranded lockout as urgent while booking a rekey or a lock install as routine. You set what counts as a drop-everything call.

03

Will it actually book the job and give an ETA?

Yes. Routine work lands straight on your calendar based on your availability, and the caller gets a realistic arrival window instead of a vague callback. For a true emergency it can book the first opening and alert you at once so you decide whether to head out right now.

04

Is it going to sound like a robot to a stranded caller?

It answers naturally and calmly, and it is upfront instead of pretending to be a person. Someone locked out in a parking lot at night mostly needs to know a real locksmith is on the way and roughly when, and a steady voice that gives an ETA beats a voicemail box every time. You can hear it handle a live call before you decide.

05

How fast can it be running?
