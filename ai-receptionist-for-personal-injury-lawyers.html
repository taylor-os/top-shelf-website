The call you miss is the case that signs with the next firm

Someone who was just in a wreck, or a family member calling from a hospital waiting room, is not going to leave a voicemail and sit tight. They call the next personal injury firm on Google, and the one after that, until a real person picks up and starts helping. In this practice more than almost any other, the firm that answers first and begins the intake is usually the one that signs the case. But you are in a deposition, standing in front of a judge, meeting another client, or asleep at 1am, so the call rolls to voicemail and a case that could be worth a great deal to your firm goes to whoever answered.

An AI receptionist answers on the first ring, stays steady with a shaken caller, finds out what happened, when, and how badly someone is hurt, and either books the consultation or flags a true emergency to your on-call attorney on the spot. The case is captured and moving instead of lost to the firm across town.

Built around how accident calls actually come in

Your calls do not keep office hours. A wreck happens on a Friday night, a fall on a Saturday, and the call often comes hours or days later, from a hospital bed, a body shop, or a spouse in a waiting room, whenever the person finally decides to find a lawyer. A voicemail box cannot run an intake, and a generic call center reading a script does not know which questions matter or which call cannot wait until Monday.

  • Answers 24/7, including the nights, weekends, and holidays when accidents happen and every other firm sends the caller to voicemail.
  • Runs real intake the way your staff would: what happened, when and where, the injuries, the other driver and their insurance, whether there is a police report, and whether they have already spoken to another firm or an adjuster.
  • Flags a true emergency, a serious injury, a caller still at the scene, a filing deadline that looks close, to your on-call attorney at once so a person can call back fast.
  • Handles a returning client or a referral differently from a first-time caller, so the people who already trust your firm never hit a machine.

The math is one signed case

You do not need to catch many calls for this to pay for itself. A single serious injury case you would have lost on a weekend is worth far more to your firm than the system costs, and the intake captured on that first call is often what decides whether the person signs with you at all. Everything it captures after that first save is on top. The point is to stop handing your best cases to the firm that simply answered faster.

You own the number, the intake, and the client list

This runs on your existing firm number, or a new one registered in your name, not ours. Every caller, every intake, and every detail is yours and exportable any time, so the leads you work hard to earn are an asset your firm keeps instead of something you rent back month to month. There is no long contract holding your data. The AI receptionist is one piece of the Top Shelf platform, and it hands every intake it captures to the same CRM that follows up, so a consultation you have not signed yet never goes cold. It answers and records the facts; your attorneys make every legal decision and every call about whether to take a case.

How it plays out

A midnight call after a wreck, captured while you sleep

It is nearly midnight when someone rear-ended on the highway gets home from the emergency room and starts searching for a personal injury lawyer. They call the first three they find. Two go to voicemail. Yours answers, stays calm, gets the date and location of the crash, the injuries, the other driver's insurance, and whether a police report was filed, then books a morning consultation and flags it to your on-call attorney as time-sensitive. You wake up to the intake already written up, with the caller's number attached, instead of hearing they signed with someone else.

Questions Personal Injury Lawyers Ask

Common Questions

01

Does it work with my current firm phone number?

Yes. It can answer on your existing number, or set up a new one registered in your name. Either way the number and every intake that comes through it belong to your firm and go with you if you ever leave.

02

Can it actually run intake, not just take a message?

Yes, that is the point. It asks the questions your intake staff would, what happened, when and where, the injuries, the other driver and their insurance, whether there is a police report, and writes it up so an attorney can pick it up cold. You decide exactly which questions it asks.

03

Will it wake up my on-call attorney for a real emergency?

It flags the calls you tell it to treat as urgent, a serious injury, a caller still at the scene, a deadline that looks close, and alerts your on-call attorney right away so a person can call back fast. You set what counts as drop-everything.

04

Is it going to give the caller legal advice?

No. It answers, reassures, and captures the facts; it does not advise on the case or promise anything about it. Every legal question and every decision about taking a case stays with your attorneys. Someone shaken after a wreck mostly needs to know a real firm is handling it, and a steady voice that gets the details beats a voicemail box.

05

How fast can it be running?
