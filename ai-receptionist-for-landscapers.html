The call you miss in spring is a whole season you hand to the next company

A homeowner who has decided to hire a lawn service does not leave one voicemail and wait. They call two or three companies and sign with whoever picks up, gets them a quote, and can start soon, because the yard is on their mind right now and they want it handled. That decision is worth far more than a single job, because a weekly mowing customer is recurring revenue across the whole season and often for years. But your crews are out on the route with mowers running, you are mid-quote in someone's backyard, or you are driving between stops, so the call rolls to voicemail and a season of work goes to whoever answered.

An AI receptionist answers on the first ring, day or night, stays friendly and professional, finds out whether it is a weekly maintenance quote, a one-off cleanup, a design or install job, or a commercial account shopping around, gets the address, and either books the estimate or flags it to you as a lead worth calling back yourself. The work is captured and scheduled instead of lost to the company down the road.

Built around how landscapers actually get calls

Your phone does not ring at a steady pace. It is quiet through the winter, then spring arrives and a season of quote calls lands in a few weeks, most of them while your crews are out on the route and nobody can hear a phone over the mowers. A voicemail box cannot triage that, and a generic call center reading a script does not know a weekly mowing signup from a full landscape install or a commercial bid worth chasing.

  • Answers every call, including the spring signup rush and the Saturday quote request off your truck sign, so a season of recurring work does not pile up in voicemail.
  • Triages like you would: weekly or biweekly maintenance, a one-off cleanup, a design or hardscape install, an irrigation issue, and how soon they need a quote.
  • Gets the address and the property details, books the estimate on your calendar, and texts you a commercial or HOA lead at once so you can call that high-value account back yourself.
  • Handles a current maintenance customer differently from a first-time caller, so the accounts you already have never hit a voicemail.

The math is one maintenance contract

You do not need to catch many calls for this to pay for itself. One weekly maintenance customer you would have lost in the spring rush is recurring revenue for the whole season, and often the next one too, so it is usually worth more than the system costs for a long time. A single commercial or HOA account is worth far more still. Everything it captures after that first save is on top. The point is to stop handing a season's worth of signups to the landscaper who simply answered faster.

You own the number, the calls, and the customer list

This runs on your existing business number, or a new one registered in your name, not ours. Every caller, every address, and every property detail is yours and exportable any time, so your customer list is an asset you keep instead of something you rent back month to month. There is no long contract holding your data hostage. The AI receptionist is one piece of the Top Shelf platform, and it hands every call it captures to the same CRM that follows up, so a quote or a seasonal reminder never slips.

How it plays out

A spring-rush Saturday, booked while you are on the route

It is a Saturday in April, the first real warm weekend of the year, and homeowners all over town are deciding at the same time to finally hire out the yard. Your two crews are on the route and the phone is ringing all day. Normally those calls stack up in voicemail and half of them never call back. Instead the AI receptionist answers every one, sorts the weekly-mowing signups from the one-off cleanups, books estimates across your open slots for the week, and texts you the single call from a property manager whose HOA landscaping contract is up for renewal so you can call that account back yourself. You did not miss the rush, and none of that recurring work went to the company down the road.

Questions Landscapers Ask

Common Questions

01

Does it work with my current phone number?

Yes. It can answer on your existing business number, or set up a new one registered in your name. Either way the number and every call that comes through it belong to you and go with you if you ever leave.

02

Can it tell a weekly maintenance customer from a one-off install?

Yes, that is the point. It asks the questions you would, weekly or biweekly mowing, a one-time cleanup, a design or hardscape install, an irrigation issue, and how soon they need a quote, so a season-long maintenance signup and a big install estimate are flagged differently before you ever call back. You set what counts as a drop-everything lead, like a commercial or HOA bid.

03

Will it actually book the estimate on my calendar?

Yes. It books the on-site estimate straight onto your calendar based on your availability, and you get a text with the details right away. For a high-value commercial or HOA lead it can flag you at once so you decide whether to call that account back yourself.

04

Can it keep up with the spring rush when every call comes at once?

That is exactly what it is for. It answers every call at the same time, so the week the whole town decides to hire a lawn service does not send half your callers to voicemail. It does not get overwhelmed, put people on hold, or clock out at five.

05

Is it going to sound like a robot to my customers?

It answers naturally and calmly, and it is upfront instead of pretending to be a person. Someone who just wants a quote on their yard mostly needs to know a real company picked up and is handling it, and that beats a voicemail box every time. You can hear it handle a live call before you decide.
