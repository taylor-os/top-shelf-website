What actually drives the price of a roofing website

A roofing website can cost almost nothing or several thousand dollars, and the range is real, so the honest answer starts with what you are actually buying. A template you fill in yourself is cheap and usually looks it. A custom site built to rank in local search, load fast, and turn a worried homeowner into a booked inspection takes real work, and that is what you are paying for when the number goes up.

The things that move the price are the number of pages, whether it is written to rank for the towns and roof types you actually work, whether it captures leads into a system that follows up, and who hosts and maintains it after launch. A brochure that just sits there is the cheapest to build and the most expensive to own, because it never books a job. A site that ranks and captures leads costs more up front and pays for itself the first storm it catches.

Cheap, free, and included are not the same thing

With Top Shelf there are two honest paths. You can buy a custom five-page site outright for $1,500 one-time, and it is yours to keep and host wherever you like, with no plan and no monthly. Or you get the website built, hosted, and included free on a monthly plan that starts at $299, which also handles your Google Business Profile, online booking, and automated review requests. There is no setup fee either way.

The distinction matters, because included free does not mean the plan is free. On a plan you are paying for the whole system that keeps the site working, the ranking, the booking, and the follow-up, and the website simply comes with it instead of as a separate bill. Buying the site outright makes sense if you only want a page you own. Getting it on a plan makes sense if you want the site to actually bring in inspections. Either way the site, the domain, and the content are in your name.
