Where a window cleaning business actually loses the account

A window cleaning business loses its most valuable work quietly, because the prize is a standing account, not a single clean. When a homeowner tired of spotted glass or a shop manager tired of fingerprints calls, they often want regular service, so a call that rings while your crew is up a ladder is not one lost job, it is a recurring account that renews for years, handed to whoever answered. The other loss is invisible: a happy one-time customer never rebooks, not because they were unhappy, but because the glass spotted up months later and they could not remember your name, so they searched and hired whoever turned up first.

Top Shelf closes both gaps. An AI receptionist answers every call even while your crew is on a ladder, asks whether it is a home or a storefront and how often they want it, and books the estimate or the recurring visit. A CRM remembers who is due and reminds every past customer at the moment the glass is spotting up again, so a one-time clean becomes a standing plan instead of a name you forgot. And your website and local SEO get you found for window cleaning near me and put the reviews that sell this trade front and center, because clean glass barely reads in a photo.

One system, not a bucket full of apps

The reason accounts slip is that answering, rebooking, and marketing sit in separate tools, and a crew working a route all day cannot run them from the top of a ladder. Top Shelf is one connected system. The visit the receptionist books lands in the same CRM that holds the storefront rotation, fires the residential rebooking reminder, and asks a happy customer for a review while the glass is gleaming. Your marketing keeps your Google profile active and stacked with recent reviews, which is what wins the click when a spotless window does not sell on a photo the way some trades do. You keep your own number and your customer list, and both leave with you if you ever go.
